## Queued

### 1. Privacy: informational privacy vs. broad personal autonomy

- **The question:** Should the privacy amendment protect *personal information* (data, communications, surveillance — the New Hampshire/Michigan model) or the broader *right to be let alone* (personal autonomy — the California/Griswold lineage)?
- **Why it matters:** Probably the single most consequential decision for the privacy category. "Right to privacy" is the doctrinal root of *Griswold*/*Roe* — post-*Dobbs*, an autonomy framing will be heard as an abortion amendment, colliding with our rejected-categories discipline. The framings that won state landslides (Michigan ~89%, New Hampshire ~81%, Missouri ~75%) were all informational and government-facing.
- **Background:** [PRIVACY_RESEARCH.md](PRIVACY_RESEARCH.md) (amendment framework, state models); [POLLING_RESEARCH.md](POLLING_RESEARCH.md) §5.
- **Status:** Queued.

## Backlog (candidate topics surfaced in working docs)

- **Electoral College:** what is the real issue other than perceived unfairness from the left? (Open question recorded in [CATEGORIES.md](CATEGORIES.md) rejected list.)
- **Qualified immunity reframe:** can "hold officers accountable for misconduct" (84%, 64% R) carry an amendment where "eliminate qualified immunity" (39–46% R) cannot? ([POLLING_RESEARCH.md](POLLING_RESEARCH.md))
- **Statutory vs. constitutional:** several executive-accountability drafts are statutes, not amendments ([AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md](AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md)) — does the foundation carry a statutory agenda alongside the amendment slate, or stay amendment-pure?
- **Pardon reform scope:** the durable bipartisan core is family/self-benefit bans and transparency; general "limit the pardon power" collapses inside the incumbent's party. Where's the line?
- **Defamation:** Ryan Ward's open thread ("let me know when you want to chat about defamation stuff") — never scoped; potential category or dead end?
- **Mutual disarmament on redistricting:** would each side's voters accept a mid-decade ban that binds their own party too? (The polling gap no one has tested.)
- **Article V reform:** should amending the Constitution be easier — and can the foundation say so without implying its own amendments can't pass under current rules? Every faction supports easier amending only until they imagine the other side's amendments; the least partisan-coded slice is clarifying constitutional-convention rules to defuse the runaway-convention fear. Surfaced by u/ObviousExit9, u/Grapetree3, and the thread OP in [REDDIT_THREAD_IDEAS.md](REDDIT_THREAD_IDEAS.md).
- **Is a limited executive-compliance amendment worth doing?** After the full-spectrum gate, the amendment is just the tiered bindingness clause plus the safe harbor — declaratory text with no enforcement machinery, because everything with teeth (appropriations fidelity, independent enforcement, defiance consequences) is unpolled or partisan-coded ([EXECUTIVE_COMPLIANCE_RESEARCH.md](EXECUTIVE_COMPLIANCE_RESEARCH.md)). The case for: the 84% (78R/92D) floor is the strongest in the platform; South Africa shows the absence of express text is the first thing a defiant executive's lawyers cite, and Germany shows binding text plus political culture works with no enforcement arm at all. The case against: the Emoluments Clauses are the domestic proof that constitutional text without a designated enforcer is a parchment barrier — a declaratory amendment could spend enormous ratification capital to change little. Middle paths to debate: ratify the declaratory core now and let enforcement rejoin by later statute/amendment as polling matures; or hold the category until the enforcement provisions are polled; or keep it as the platform's "flagship valence" amendment whose function is partly coalition-building for the rest of the slate. Question logged 2026-08-12 (Michael Hamilton).
