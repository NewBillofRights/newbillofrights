## Findings in detail

### 1. `ethics-forced-divestment` — the audit's headline

The rejection rests on a 26% overall Bloomberg/Selzer 2016 reading with no party split, plus staleness and blast-radius objections. The original pass missed that the corpus's own civil-service source — the UMD Program for Public Consultation questionnaire of May 19–30, 2023 (n=2,625 RV, ±1.9) — carries the divestment design at earlier question numbers. **Verified firsthand from the cached PDF** ([questionnaire](https://publicconsultation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/StockTradesQuaire_0623.pdf), cached 2026-08-19):

- **Q6** — Congress and resident family barred from individual stocks; existing holdings must be **sold or placed in a blind trust** (mutual/index funds still allowed): favor **86.2% — 86.6% R / 88.3% D / 80.5% I**.
- **Q8** — the identical sell-or-blind-trust requirement extended to the **President, Vice President, and Supreme Court Justices**: approve **87.3% — 86.9% R / 90.0% D**.
- **Q4** — the costs argument ("having to sell stocks or put them in a blind trust could cost them a significant amount of money or lose key opportunities") was found convincing by **25.7%** (24.9% R / 23.3% D) — and the Q6/Q8 votes came *after* respondents heard it. This substantially satisfies the path back's costs-named structure.

What remains unfielded is the path back's precise question: **sale-only** isolated against the blind-trust alternative. The 2016 Selzer floor, the no-party-split objection, and the staleness objection no longer describe the evidence; the abuse-vs-remedy objection is answered for the two-option design and untested for sale-only. **Recommendation: add the sale-only vs blind-trust head-to-head to the commissioned survey spec; whether the entry moves back to candidate on the PPC evidence alone is the founder's call.** (For scale: these numbers match the ethics category's own 86–88% floor anchors.)

### 2. `civic-information` — one leg of two

The recorded path back requires both a 60% floor in the less-supportive party *and* a demonstrated statutory failure. The second leg now has documentation the corpus lacked: GAO found agencies persistently miss FOIA's 20-working-day deadline, backlog-reduction plans lacked goals and timelines, and processing-time reporting has been inaccurate since 2013 ([GAO-24-106535](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106535)); FY2024 set a record ~1.5M requests with simple-track processing at 44 days, the worst since 2011 ([DOJ OIP summary via ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/doj-oip-foia-requests-fy-2024)); and the April 2025 HHS reduction-in-force eliminated the CDC's FOIA office outright ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/hhs-firings-federal-health-agencies-foia-requests-public-records/)). The polling leg remains empty: no independent national poll with party splits has ever asked the general right-to-records question. The nearest adjacent reading — release of the Epstein investigation files, **67% R / 91% D** ([Economist/YouGov, Nov 15–17, 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53427-bipartisan-majorities-want-government-to-release-jeffrey-epstein-records-november-15-17-2025-economist-yougov-poll)) — shows a records-transparency demand clearing 60% R but is issue-specific. **Recommendation: the general right-to-records question is now worth a slot in the commissioned instrument; both legs would then be measurable.**

### 3. `statehood` — the mechanism split is real, the threshold untested

A missed Washington Post–University of Maryland poll (Oct 9–14, 2019, n=860 Maryland residents) fielded exactly the grievance/remedy split the path back specifies: a full voting House member for D.C. drew **54% R support**, against **31% R** for statehood and 30% R for retrocession in the same instrument ([crosstabs](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1tTOvoF-viobGeDStb8ZXK7bUCYmsWH0KE3jNyDDSoOw/edit#gid=0)). The 23-point Republican gap between remedy and statehood confirms the path back's design intuition — but the sample is Maryland-only, 54% is below the 60% R threshold, and no national pollster has fielded the split in 2021–2026. A 2005 KRC/DC Vote figure (82% for voting representation) is advocacy-commissioned and unverifiable at source — *verify before use*.

### 4. `privacy-deletion-right` — instrument guidance, not a reopening

The recorded path back stands unmet: no one has fielded the records-excluding constitutional deletion right. But the original floor (30% R, Pew 2019, deletion applied to law-enforcement records) partly reflects the word *delete* and the law-enforcement framing rather than blanket hostility to relief over government records. UMD PPC's deliberative criminal-records survey (Feb 12–22, 2021, n=2,487 RV) read **sealing** records of the never-convicted at **69.7% R / 84.7% D** and automatic sealing of non-violent drug records at **67.9% R / 83.6% D** — both after respondents heard con arguments ([questionnaire](https://publicconsultation.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Criminal_Records_Quaire_0221.pdf)). The seal-from-public vs erase-the-government's-copy distinction maps onto the records-excluding line the path back's Step 1 draws. **Recommendation: if the Step 2 question is ever commissioned, field "sealed from public view" alongside "deleted" as a split sample; the 2019 gradient may be a vocabulary artifact.**

### 5. `ethics-civil-service-coverage` — a path back could now be written

No path back is recorded. The audit found the reason the clause fails is breadth-specific, measured: Morning Consult/Politico (Oct 21–23, 2022, n=2,005 RV) read a trading ban for **"top officials working for agencies in the federal government" at 66% R / 66% D / 69% I** (and Federal Reserve top officials at 67/67) ([crosstabs](https://pro-assets.morningconsult.com/wp-uploads/2022/10/25151347/2210148_crosstabs_POLITICO_RVs_v2_10-26-22_SH.pdf)), while the corpus's PPC Q12 all-employees reading sits at 41.6% R / 37.1% D. PPC's own 2024 re-field dropped the all-employees item ([Swing Six](https://publicconsultation.org/swing-six/stock-trading/)). **Recommendation: if a path back is written, key it to a senior-officials vs all-employees split-sample rather than waiting for all-employees polling no one fields.** The no-why-an-amendment objection is untouched by any of this.

### 6. `election-technology` — one component is the opposite of unpopular

The category's 15% R / 39% D floor came from an online-voting/modernization framing. The paper-record mandate polls in a different universe: requiring electronic voting machines to print a paper backup reads **85% R / 82% D** (Pew ATP, Jan 16–21, 2024, n=5,140; [report](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/02/07/bipartisan-support-for-early-in-person-voting-voter-id-election-day-national-holiday/)) and **87% R / 82% D** (Pew ATP, Aug 4–10, 2025, n=3,554; [report](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/08/22/majority-of-americans-continue-to-back-expanded-early-voting-voting-by-mail-voter-id/) — **re-verified firsthand at the report page**). Two waves, two cycles, Republicans above Democrats both times. This does not disturb the category rejection — the statutory objection is untouched — but it strengthens the rejected `voting-rights` entry's paired wording, which includes "a paper record of the ballot": its strongest component now has a stable 82%+ floor in *both* parties. Cross-referenced there.

### 7. Rejections actively strengthened by the audit

- **`pardon-congressional-disapproval`.** The missed Aug 15–19, 2024 wave of the identical YouGov item ("giving Congress the power to reverse a presidential pardon") read **26% R / 42% D** ([crosstabs](https://ygo-assets-websites-editorial-emea.yougov.net/documents/Presidential_Pardons_poll_results.pdf)) against the corpus's 2026 reading of 30% R / **73% D** — a 31-point Democratic swing on unchanged wording as the White House changed hands, while the Republican number stayed in the 20s–30s. That is the abuse-vs-remedy dynamic measured directly, and it predicts the path back's symmetry follow-up would fail. A softer generic "amend to limit the pardon power" item read 34% R / 76% D even at peak salience ([Economist/YouGov, Dec 5–8, 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53702-majorities-americans-say-donald-trump-gives-too-many-pardons-want-to-limit-pardon-power-december-5-8-2025-economist-yougov-poll)). Design note for the record: a Supreme-Court-review variant slightly outpolled the congressional version (50% vs 48% overall) on the same 2026 battery — the distrust may attach to Congress specifically.
- **`filibuster-reform`.** The newest wave, fielded under the flipped (Republican) majority, shows the parties re-sorting yet again: Republican support for the 60-vote rule fell 51→31 and Democratic opposition fell 56→24, with 48% now unsure ([Economist/YouGov, Oct 17–20, 2025, Q32](https://d3nkl3psvxxpe9.cloudfront.net/documents/econTabReport_yAo1neA.pdf)). The inversion design the path back specifies has never been fielded; the nearest item ("cannot block with a threat alone") hit 45% R in June 2022 but 35% R in March 2021 under the same majority — unstable even within one regime. One genuine note: the **talking filibuster** cleared 45% in both parties in both 2021 and 2022 waves, but both were under a Democratic majority and it is a milder design than the recorded path back.
