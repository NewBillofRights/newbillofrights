# Voting Rights & Election Access — Rejected Category Memo

*Rejected 2026-08-06. Memo written 2026-08-17 to substantiate the rejection under [SELECTION_METHOD.md](../SELECTION_METHOD.md). Every figure below was verified against the linked source on the date of writing.*

**Verdict in one line:** 32% (Republicans) on no-excuse mail voting, Pew August 2025 — Weak; the affirmative right-to-vote amendment itself is unpolled — fails high-topline/low-floor on every access mechanism and every verification mechanism except photo ID and early in-person voting, is regime/salience-coded (Democratic support for photo ID fell from 71% to 51% across the 2026 SAVE America Act fight), and fails the symmetry test because each party's package is written against the other's — path back: poll one paired proposal (an ID guarantee and an access guarantee in the same clause) and clear a 60% floor in both parties.

## 1. The proposal as commonly framed

The Constitution contains no affirmative right to vote; it forbids denial "on account of" race, sex, age, and poll taxes, and leaves the rest to states and Congress. The category as it enters this pipeline is therefore an **affirmative-right amendment**: "Every citizen of the United States, who is of legal voting age, shall have the fundamental right to vote in any public election held in the jurisdiction in which the citizen resides," with an enforcement clause for Congress — the text of the Pocan–Ellison [H.J.Res. 25 (114th Congress, January 2015)](https://pocan.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/pocan-and-ellison-to-introduce-right-to-vote-constitutional-amendment). The Senate version is Sen. Durbin's [S.J.Res. 77 (118th, introduced May 7, 2024, 8 cosponsors)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/77) and [S.J.Res. 186 (119th, introduced April 27, 2026, 9 cosponsors: 8 Democrats, 1 independent, no Republicans)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/186/cosponsors). Neither has had a hearing.

Two statutory packages define what each party would actually put behind such a clause. The Democratic package (For the People Act, John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act, Freedom to Vote Act) guarantees automatic and same-day registration, no-excuse mail voting, early-voting minimums, and restored preclearance. The Republican package (SAVE Act 2025, SAVE America Act 2026) requires documentary proof of citizenship to register and government photo ID to vote, with DHS roll matching. A third variant — the state constitutional model — puts specific mechanisms in state constitutions by ballot measure (Michigan Proposal 2, 2022; Wisconsin Question 1, 2025).

Pipeline entry: **Ryan Ward** named "voting rights" as a next-tier target (specifics not discussed) — [AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md](../AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md). The founder's one-line rejection: "access-vs-integrity framing splits cleanly along party lines."

## 2. The evidence

**Independent polls with party breakdowns.** Fieldwork dates and question paraphrases as published.

- No-excuse mail voting ("allow any voter to vote by mail if they want to"): **58% overall, 32% R / 83% D** — [Pew, Aug 4–10, 2025 (n=3,554)](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/08/22/majority-of-americans-continue-to-back-expanded-early-voting-voting-by-mail-voter-id/). In April 2020 the same item was 70% (49% R / 87% D); the 51-point gap is Pew's widest on any voting item. Gallup's version ("send absentee ballot applications to all eligible voters"): **60%, 36% R / 63% I / 87% D** — [Gallup, Oct 1–12, 2024, topline & tabs](https://news.gallup.com/file/poll/652532/2024_10_23_Election%20Laws%20Topline%20and%20Tabs.pdf).
- Automatic voter registration: **59%, 42% R / 77% D** — Pew Aug 2025; **60%, 43% R / 60% I / 79% D** — Gallup Oct 2024.
- Same-day (Election Day) registration: **58%, 43% R / 72% D** — Pew Aug 2025 (Republican support was 49% in 2018 — [Pew, Jan 16–21, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/02/07/bipartisan-support-for-early-in-person-voting-voter-id-election-day-national-holiday/)).
- Proof of citizenship to register: the mirror image. Gallup Oct 2024, "requiring people who are registering to vote for the first time to provide proof of citizenship": **83%, 96% R / 84% I / 66% D**. Eighteen months later, with the SAVE America Act on the Senate floor, NPR/PBS News/Marist, Apr 27–30, 2026 (n=1,322), "requiring people to show proof of citizenship, such as a birth certificate or passport, in order to register to vote": **65%, 93% R / 67% I / 37% D** — [Marist tables (PDF)](https://maristpoll.marist.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NPR_PBS-News_Marist-Poll_USA-NOS-and-Tables_202605011058.pdf). Yahoo News/YouGov, Feb 9–12, 2026 (n=1,704): **62% favor / 23% oppose; 89% R; 39% D favor, 45% D oppose** — [Yahoo News write-up (the poll's publisher)](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/article/poll-as-the-2026-midterm-elections-approach-just-23-of-americans-are-confident-the-vote-will-not-be-rigged-221139207.html).
- Government-issued photo ID to vote: **83%, 95% R / 71% D** — Pew Aug 2025 (Democratic support 61% in 2023, 69% in 2024 — Pew Jan 2024); **84%, 98% R / 84% I / 67% D** — Gallup Oct 2024 (Democrats up 14 points from 2022); **74%, 93% R / 77% I / 51% D** — Marist Apr 2026 (binary format, no "unsure" offered). As a *constitutional amendment* — "Require photo ID to vote" — **66% support / 21% oppose; 58% D / 61% I / 80% R (Dem 34% oppose)** — [Cato/Morning Consult, June 25–26, 2026, crosstabs Table CATO20_5 (n=1,208 for this item)](https://www.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/2026-07/Cato%20Fourth%20of%20July%202026%20Survey%20Crosstabs.pdf).
- Early in-person voting for at least two weeks: **80%, 71% R / 89% D** — Pew Aug 2025 (Republican support "has grown since last year"). Gallup's looser "early voting" item: **76%, 57% R / 76% I / 95% D** — Oct 2024, with Republican support down from 74% in 2016.
- Restoring the vote to people with felony convictions after their sentence: **66%, 56% R / 77% D** — Pew Aug 2025.
- Election Day as a national holiday: **74%, 74% R / 76% D** — Pew Aug 2025 (68% R / 78% D in Jan 2024).
- Paper backup for electronic voting machines: **84%, 87% R / 82% D** — Pew Aug 2025.
- Roll maintenance and ballot handling — the two items with more opposition than support: removing registrants who have not voted recently or confirmed their registration **43%, 63% R / 24% D**; banning third-party collection of completed ballots **45%, 55% R / 37% D** — Pew Aug 2025. Gallup Oct 2024: purge after five years' non-voting **35%, 51% R / 36% I / 16% D**; limit drop boxes **40%, 61% R / 37% I / 20% D**.
- The framing split measured directly (forced choice — not a favor/oppose number): "more concerned with ensuring that everyone who wants to vote can do so" vs. "making sure no one votes who is ineligible": **59 / 41 overall; Democrats 86 / 14; Republicans 30 / 70; independents 53 / 47** — [NPR/PBS News/Marist, Mar 2–4, 2026 (n=1,591)](https://maristpoll.marist.edu/polls/election-security-march-2026/).

**Ballot results** (official results as compiled by Ballotpedia; certified).

- Standalone photo-ID amendments pass, including in swing states: Nevada Question 7 (Nov 2024, first of two required votes) **73%** ([Ballotpedia 2024 results](https://ballotpedia.org/Results_for_voting-related_and_noncitizen_voting_ballot_measures,_2024)); Wisconsin Question 1 (Apr 1, 2025) **62.8%** ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Wisconsin_Question_1,_Require_Voter_Photo_ID_Amendment_(April_2025))); Nebraska Initiative 432 (2022) 65.5%; Missouri Amendment 6 (2016) 63.0%.
- ID **bundled with access restrictions** loses: Maine Question 1 (Nov 2025 — photo ID plus new absentee and drop-box limits) **35.8% yes / 64.2% no** ([Ballotpedia 2025 results](https://ballotpedia.org/Results_for_election_and_voting-related_ballot_measures,_2025)); Arizona Proposition 309 (2022 — ID for mail ballots and in-person voting) **49.6 / 50.4** (Ballotpedia, Wisconsin Q1 page table).
- Citizens-only voting amendments pass by wide margins in every state that has voted, red and blue: Nov 2024 — Idaho 65%, Iowa 77%, Kentucky 62%, Missouri 68%, North Carolina 78%, Oklahoma 81%, South Carolina 86%, Wisconsin 71%; Texas Proposition 16 (2025) 72%; Colorado Amendment 76 (2020) 63%; Florida Amendment 1 (2020) 79% (Ballotpedia 2024 and 2025 results pages).

**Advocacy-commissioned polls.** None are needed for the verdict; the independent series is dense and consistent. (The corpus caution-list line "photo ID 66% (coded right)" traces to the Cato/Morning Consult June 2026 poll — an ideological think tank's commission with a probability-based vendor — and its crosstabs are used above only for the amendment-framing point.)

**Nuances & caveats**

- Cross-pollster comparisons carry format effects: Marist forces a binary (Democrats 51% on photo ID), Pew offers a four-point scale (71%). The direction of the 2025→2026 movement, however, is the same in every 2026 series (Marist, Yahoo/YouGov, Cato) and coincides with the SAVE America Act floor fight — a salience effect, not a settled Democratic majority against ID.
- Within-pollster trends are regime-coded: Republican mail-voting support 49% → 32% (Pew, 2020→2025) tracks the 2020 dispute; Republican early-voting support 74% → 57% (Gallup, 2016→2024); Democratic photo-ID support 61% → 71% (Pew, 2023→2025) then down at the moment the policy became a Republican bill.
- Every access mechanism except early voting and the holiday sits at 32–43% Republican; every verification mechanism except photo ID sits at 16–37% Democratic. Voters in *both* parties reject the enforcement machinery of the other side (purges 24% D; ballot-collection bans 37% D; only 55–63% even among Republicans).
- Real ballots confirm the pattern rather than soften it: standalone ID and citizenship clauses pass everywhere; ID bundled with access limits lost in Maine and Arizona; access packages pass in swing states only with an ID-or-affidavit clause attached (Michigan).
- Independents sit at the national average on every Gallup item — the split is a two-party split, not a broad public one.

## 3. The gate result

- **Bipartisan floor:** **32% (R)** on no-excuse mail voting, Pew Aug 2025 → **Weak** per §4.1. The access package's other mechanisms are Weak as well (42% R automatic registration, 43% R same-day). The verification package mirrors it: proof of citizenship **37% (D)**, Marist Apr 2026 → Weak. The affirmative right-to-vote amendment as drafted (Durbin/Pocan) has never been polled by an independent pollster → **Unpolled**.
- **Failure mode(s) (§4.3):** *High-topline / low-floor* — 58–60% overall for mail voting and automatic registration hides 32–43% R; 62–83% for proof of citizenship hides 37–66% D depending on the month. *Incumbent / regime coding* — R mail-voting support halved after 2020; D photo-ID support fell 20 points across pollsters when ID became a Republican floor vote; each side's floor moves with who is proposing. *Framing dependency* — the framings that clear (photo ID as policy, early voting, holiday, paper trail) are not the amendment either party would draft, and the amendment framing itself costs 13 points of Democratic support on ID (71% policy → 58% amendment).
- **Non-polling tests (§5):** *Existential-problem* — arguably passes; confidence that elections will be fair fell to 66% (Marist Mar 2026). *Symmetry* — **fails.** Each party's package is a remedy for the other party's conduct: the D package restores preclearance and overrides state ID/roll rules; the R package imposes citizenship documentation and DHS matching on all states. Neither can be sold with paired abuses because each side denies the other's premise (fraud vs. suppression), and the Marist forced-choice shows the premises themselves split 86/14 vs. 30/70. *Why-an-amendment* — passes narrowly (the text is silent; *Bush v. Gore* confirmed no affirmative federal right; statutes have been struck — *Shelby County v. Holder* (2013) held the Voting Rights Act's preclearance coverage formula unconstitutional — or filibustered), but this test cannot rescue a category that fails the gate and symmetry.

## 4. Framings tested

- **Affirmative right to vote (Durbin/Pocan text).** Unpolled by any independent pollster; sponsor list is single-party (8 D + 1 I, 0 R on S.J.Res. 186). Cannot clear on evidence available.
- **Universal mail voting / automatic registration / same-day registration.** 32–43% R (Pew 2025; Gallup 2024). Weak. Do not clear.
- **Proof of citizenship to register.** 66% D at low salience (Gallup Oct 2024) → 37–39% D during the 2026 floor fight (Marist, Yahoo/YouGov). Regime-coded; the low-salience number is not the number an amendment campaign would face. Does not clear.
- **Photo ID to vote.** Clears as *policy* on the pre-2026 series (67–71% D floor, Gallup 2024 / Pew 2025 — Strong); Moderate on the 2026 series (51% D, Marist; 58% D as an amendment, Cato). Standalone state amendments pass 63–73% in swing states. Honest read: Moderate and unstable — the same mechanism the SAVE America Act made a party-line vote. Not the thing this category would draft on its own, and drafting a mechanism violates "principles, not machinery" (§6.2).
- **Early in-person voting (≥2 weeks).** 71% R / 89% D (Pew 2025) — Strong; 57% R on Gallup's looser wording (2024). Clears, but as a mechanism.
- **Election Day holiday** (74% R / 76% D) and **paper ballot backup** (87% R / 82% D), Pew 2025 — Strong. Both clear; both are statutory administration, not constitutional principle.
- **Felon re-enfranchisement after sentence.** 56% R / 77% D (Pew 2025) — Moderate; Florida 2018 64.6%. Does not reach Strong.
- **Citizens-only voting clause.** Passes 62–86% in eleven states including Colorado; no independent national poll with party splits located. Already federal law for federal elections; adds nothing that fails the gate but also nothing the category is about.
- **The paired package (ID guarantee + access guarantee in one clause).** Never polled as one proposal by anyone. The nearest real test is Michigan Prop 2 (ID-or-affidavit + nine days' early voting, 60% in a swing state), but an affidavit alternative is not the ID requirement the verification side asks for; the nearest negative test is Maine 2025, where ID plus access limits lost 64–36. No framing on the record clears the bar as an amendment either party would sign.

## 5. Path back

Commission, with an independent pollster, a single proposal worded roughly: *"Every citizen has the right to vote in federal elections; states may require free government-issued photo identification, and every citizen shall have at least two weeks of early in-person voting and a paper record of the ballot"* — tested as one favor/oppose item, at a low-salience moment, and re-tested after the next change in House control. Reopen if the **lower party's support is ≥60%** on the paired wording and the gap between the two parties is under 20 points in two independent polls (§4.1, §11 forward-looking rule). A second trigger: a Republican- and Democrat-cosponsored joint resolution with the paired text receiving a committee hearing, or a swing-state ballot measure pairing an ID requirement (not an affidavit alternative) with an early-voting guarantee passing at 60%+. Until one of those exists, the category stays rejected — not because voting does not matter, but because every version on the record codes the whole project as one party's.

## Review Additions (2026-08-17)

*Added after an outside completeness review; every item below was verified against the linked source before inclusion.*

- Corrected: the §2 legislative-votes lead-in said "every federal package of the last five years has been a party-line vote." Qualified to voter-access and voter-verification packages, because the Electoral Count Reform and Presidential Transition Improvement Act of 2022 — enacted as Division P of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 (H.R. 2617, Pub. L. 117-328, Dec. 29, 2022; [govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-117publ328/html/PLAW-117publ328.htm)) — passed the Senate **68–29 (R 18–20, D 49–0, I 2–0)** on Dec. 22, 2022 ([Senate Vote 421](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1172/vote_117_2_00421.xml)) and the House **225–201 (R 9–200, D 216–1)** on Dec. 23, 2022 ([Clerk, Roll 549](https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2022/roll549.xml)). Both votes were on the omnibus, so they measure the package rather than the ECRA alone; the ECRA governs the counting of electoral votes and the Vice President's role in it, not registration, ballot access or voter verification. No change to the gate result.
- ***Shelby County v. Holder*, 570 U.S. 529 (June 25, 2013)** — held that "Section 4 of the Voting Rights Act is unconstitutional; its formula can no longer be used as a basis for subjecting jurisdictions to preclearance" — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/12-96). This is the struck statute behind the Democratic package's preclearance-restoration provisions (John Lewis VRAA) and is now named in §3's why-an-amendment line. It does not change the §5.3 result (passes narrowly) or the rejection, which rests on the gate and the symmetry test.
