# Marketing & Positioning Ideas

Working list of ideas about how the project is presented — branding, framing, sequencing — with
contributor attribution. Kept separate from [AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md](AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md) (substance)
so that positioning never quietly drives which amendments make the list: the full-spectrum test in
[SELECTION_METHOD.md](SELECTION_METHOD.md) decides that, and only that.

## Ideas

- **Alex MacGregor (text message, 2026-08-18) — Get to ten amendments, to mirror the Bill of
  Rights.** "I think it's better branding, and is presented as a block rather than something that
  can be added and deleted willy nilly." The block of ten reads as a coherent charter, invites the
  obvious comparison, and resists the impression of a running list. **Michael's response:** open to
  it, but the count has to be earned — "I don't have 10 qualified yet." Getting from eight to ten
  means commissioning polling with party breakdowns on the candidate ideas that have potential but
  no proof (see the candidate list in [CATEGORIES.md](CATEGORIES.md) and
  [AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md](AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md)); Michael estimates two more areas need to be fleshed
  out. Until then the site presents eight and says so.
- **Alex MacGregor (same conversation) — Positioning is the whole game.** Getting an amendment
  through Article V depends on how it is positioned politically "probably more than anything": it
  has to be specifically nonpartisan and avoid language coded to either side, or one media
  ecosystem will characterize it as the other side's project immediately. **Michael's response:**
  this is already the house rule — see the nonpartisan-discipline section of
  [COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md](../COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md) (no side-coded vocabulary, no officeholder names,
  paired examples, party breakdown on every number).
