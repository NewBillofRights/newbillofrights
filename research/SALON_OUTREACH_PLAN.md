# Los Angeles Salon Outreach Plan

*Working plan for [Roadmap step 4](ROADMAP.md) — the monthly in-person Los Angeles salon. Drafted 2026-08-18. Companion to [SALON_TOPICS.md](SALON_TOPICS.md) (what we discuss) and the public page at `/get-involved/salons`. This is an internal working document, not public copy; the invitation language in §7 is public-facing and follows [COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md](../COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md).*

## 0. What the salon is for

The salon has one job in the method (research → polling → **salon** → draft): put a real open question in front of people who disagree, in person, and let the argument change a decision. Everything else it produces is a by-product — but the by-products are the reason to run outreach carefully:

| By-product | Who produces it | Where it lands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substantive critique of a draft or memo | constitutional-law and political-science faculty, appellate/public-law practitioners | `research/*_RESEARCH.md` Review Additions; [AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md](AMENDMENT_IDEAS.md) with attribution |
| Cross-spectrum credibility ("people from both sides were in the room") | politically active attendees from each side | salon notes; eventually the public salons page |
| Volunteers (research, analytics, polling, salon organizing, fundraising) | everyone | `/get-involved/volunteer` form |
| Founding donors ($1,000+) and founding-board candidates | practitioners, donors, senior figures | `/get-involved/seed-donors` form; the invite-only online series |
| Recruiters who can bring *their* side next month | party clubs, advocacy groups, student chapters | the invite list |

The bar for the room is the same as the bar for the amendments: if the room is not genuinely full-spectrum, the salon has failed regardless of who showed up.

## 1. Design of the room (drives who we invite)

- **Size:** 14–24 seated. Below 12 the disagreement thins out; above ~25 it stops being a conversation and becomes a panel.
- **Composition target per salon** (self-reported at RSVP; see §8): roughly a third who identify right-of-center, a third left-of-center, a third centrist/independent/unaffiliated — with a hard floor that neither of the two sides is under 25% of the room. If a week out the RSVPs breach the floor, the remaining invitations go only to the under-represented side, and we say so to the people we invite ("we're short on conservatives/progressives this month; you'd be helping us keep the room honest").
- **Roles in every room:** one **legal discussant** (a con-law or election-law scholar or an appellate/public-law practitioner), one **empirical discussant** (a political scientist or pollster who can speak to the party-breakdown numbers), one **politically active discussant from each side** (someone who has actually run, organized, or served — they say what a proposal sounds like on the ground), and a **moderator** (Michael at first; rotate later). The rest of the room is the mix.
- **Format:** 6:30 arrival, 7:00–8:45 discussion, one topic, pre-read sent five days ahead (the amendment page plus a two-page brief of the open question), Chatham House rule, no recording, no slides. Decision-oriented close: "what did this change?" — recorded in [SALON_TOPICS.md](SALON_TOPICS.md) within 48 hours.
- **Cadence:** monthly, same week each month (proposal: third Wednesday), first three dates fixed before any invitation goes out so recruiters can plan.
- **Free to attend.** No money is collected at salons; the founding-donor conversation happens one-to-one afterward (§6). Until incorporation, invitations say "the New Bill of Rights project" — the foundation does not yet exist and the site says so.

## 2. Constituencies and what each one wants

Outreach only works if the invitation answers "why would I spend an evening on this?" for the specific person. Four constituencies, four different answers:

| Constituency | What they get from the salon | What we want from them | Risk to manage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Academics** (con law, election law, constitutional design, public opinion, American institutions) | A live audience arguing about their subject; draft text to critique; a nonpartisan setting that costs them nothing reputationally; a place their students can go | Critique of memos and draft text; introductions to colleagues on the other side; letting a student chapter co-host | Faculty treat it as an undergrad-level advocacy event and decline. Antidote: lead with the method (bipartisan-floor measurement, published rejections) and with a specific unresolved question, not with the amendments. |
| **Practicing lawyers** (appellate, public law, election law, government ethics, privacy/data) | Substantive constitutional argument outside billable work; a network across the bar; a project with a plausible path to a board seat | Drafting review; bar-section co-hosting; founding donors and directors; pro bono for incorporation/501(c)(3) | Being seen as one bar faction's project. Antidote: pair a Federalist Society-affiliated and an ACS-affiliated practitioner in the same room from salon one. |
| **Politically active people and organizations** (party clubs and county parties, single-issue reform groups, former officials, commissioners, consultants) | Their issue treated seriously *and* held to a bar their opponents also accept; a room where the other side is present and civil; visibility for their organization | Recruit their side; public appreciation; test what a proposal sounds like on the ground; introductions to donors | Capture. One side's org co-hosts, and the room becomes theirs. Antidote: party or ideological orgs are only ever engaged in matched pairs (§4); single-issue groups are told plainly that their issue may fail the bar. |
| **Conveners** (cross-partisan dialogue orgs, civic institutions, venues with series) | Content that fits their mission; a partner who does the substantive prep | Venue, mailing lists, and a room that is already balanced | Their format overwhelms ours (panel, audience Q&A). Antidote: co-brand a special session, but keep the monthly salon ours. |

## 3. Phased plan

Dates assume the first salon is held in **October 2026** (privacy topic, already queued) — about seven weeks from today, the minimum for a genuinely balanced first room.

### Phase 0 — Prepare (weeks of Aug 18 – Sep 5)

- Fix three dates and a room for Oct/Nov/Dec. Publish them on `/get-involved/salons` and open the Luma RSVP (cross-listed on Eventbrite). The RSVP form asks name, email, affiliation (optional), how they heard of us, and the self-placement question in §8.
- Write the **one-page brief** for the privacy question (from [SALON_TOPICS.md](SALON_TOPICS.md) §1 and PRIVACY_RESEARCH.md) — this is the pre-read and also the attachment for academic invitations.
- Write the **project one-pager** (mission, method, the eight, the ten rejected, what we ask) — one page, voice-guide compliant, Gemini-reviewed before use.
- Build the **outreach tracker** (§8) and load the target lists from [SALON_OUTREACH_TARGETS.md](SALON_OUTREACH_TARGETS.md).
- Recruit a **host committee of 4–6** before any cold outreach: ideally two lawyers (one from each side of the bar), one academic, one politically active person from each side. Their names on the invitation are what make cold outreach work. Warm paths first: MIT TPP and Georgia Tech alumni in LA; Michael's clean-energy and project-development network (regulatory lawyers there are public-law lawyers); Alex MacGregor and Ryan Ward (already contributors in the research docs); the LAUSD parent network.

### Phase 1 — Seed the room (Sep 8 – Sep 26)

- Personal, individually written invitations to the **anchor list** (§4, tier A): 25–35 people, expecting 8–12 yeses. Every invitation names the specific question, attaches the brief, and asks for one thing (attend, or if not, 20 minutes to critique the brief). Academic invitations go from Michael with a host-committee academic cc'd where possible.
- Ask each yes for **one name from the other side** ("who would argue with you about this?"). This is the single most productive recruiting question and it self-balances the room.
- Confirm the four discussant roles for salon one.

### Phase 2 — Institutional partners (Sep 22 – Oct 10)

- Approach partner organizations in **matched pairs** (§4, tier B): student chapters (Federalist Society + ACS at UCLA Law, then USC Gould), lawyer chapters, and the cross-partisan conveners (Braver Angels, LWV, Zócalo). Ask: post the salon to members, send two to four people, and — for student chapters — co-host one salon per semester on campus.
- Approach the **politically active organizations** in matched pairs (§5): county parties' clubs, party-adjacent clubs, single-issue groups. The ask is to send two people and, for the single-issue groups, to send someone who can speak to why their framing polls the way it does.
- Bar sections: get on the program calendar of the two or three most relevant sections for a lunch talk in Q1 2027; that is a slower channel and it feeds the salon rather than the reverse.

### Phase 3 — Hold, then widen (Oct – Dec)

- Salon 1 (privacy). Debrief in SALON_TOPICS.md; thank-you note within 48 hours to every attendee with the decision recorded and the next date; ask each attendee for one name for next month.
- Salon 2 and 3 pull the next queued questions. Rotate the discussants; keep the mix rule. By salon 3 the target is that half the room came through someone other than Michael.
- After salon 2: first bar-section or campus co-hosted session on the calendar; first "outer ring" outreach (Orange County, Pasadena/Claremont, Ventura).

### Phase 4 — Convert (rolling from salon 1)

- Within a week of each salon, one-to-one follow-ups sorted by the ask ladder in §6. Volunteers go straight to the `/get-involved/volunteer` form and get a real task inside two weeks (the research corpus has a standing queue: candidate memos, review additions, polling table checks). Prospective founding donors get the founding-donor conversation, not a form.

## 6. The ask ladder

Never ask for money at the salon. The ladder, in order, and each rung is asked one-to-one:

1. **Come.** (And bring one person from the other side.)
2. **Read one thing and tell us what's wrong with it.** A memo, a draft, a page. Recorded with attribution in the research docs — that is a real credit and academics and lawyers value it.
3. **Discuss.** Take a discussant seat at a future salon.
4. **Volunteer.** One of the five areas on `/get-involved/volunteer`. Lawyers: incorporation and 501(c)(3) filing pro bono is the most valuable single contribution available right now.
5. **Found.** Founding donor ($1,000+) and the founding board. Practitioners with a public-law practice, senior figures who have served on commissions, and donors with a governance-reform history are the natural pool. Cross-spectrum balance applies to the board too, and should be said out loud.

## 7. Invitation language

Public-facing; follows the voice guide (no coded vocabulary, no officeholder names, hedged claims, "we" only for our decisions). Templates, to be personalized every time — the first two sentences must be about the recipient's work.

**Academic (cold, email, ~150 words):**

> Subject: An open question about a privacy amendment — Oct [date], Los Angeles
>
> Dear Professor [ ],
>
> Your work on [specific paper/argument] is why I'm writing. I run the New Bill of Rights project, a nonpartisan effort to identify constitutional amendments that clear a support floor in *both* parties — measured, not hoped for — and to publish the ones that fail alongside the ones that pass. Once a month in Los Angeles we put one genuinely open question in front of a small room drawn deliberately from across the spectrum.
>
> October's question is whether a privacy amendment should protect personal information (the New Hampshire and Michigan model) or the broader right to be let alone. The one-page brief is attached. I'd value your critique of it more than anything else — and if the evening of [date] works, a seat at the table.
>
> [signature, site link, host committee names]

**Practitioner (warm-ish, email or LinkedIn, ~100 words):** lead with the case or practice area, then the same middle paragraph, then: "The room is half lawyers and deliberately split across the bar; [name] from [org] and [name] from [org] are both coming."

**Politically active organization (email to the program or events contact):** lead with their issue and how it did against the bar ("term limits clears it with wide margins in both parties; the general 'limit the pardon power' framing does not — the family- and self-benefit bans do"), say plainly that the room is built to have the other side present, ask for two attendees and, if useful, a discussant.

**Convener / venue partner:** what we bring (a prepared brief, a balanced list, moderation), what we ask (room, list, one co-branded session).

Two lines that go in every version, in some form: *"We are not asking anyone to endorse anything,"* and *"Everything we publish shows its sources and its party breakdowns."*

## 8. Tracking and the balance rule

- **Tracker:** one Google Sheet (or Airtable), one row per person: name · affiliation · constituency (§2) · source (who introduced) · lean (self-reported at RSVP, or "unknown") · tier · status (identified / invited / replied / RSVP / attended / follow-up sent) · ask-ladder rung · notes · date last touched. Organizations get their own tab with contact path and the matched-pair partner.
- **Self-placement question at RSVP** (optional, one line): "For balance in the room, where would you place yourself? — Right of center / Left of center / Center or independent / Prefer not to say." Only the aggregate is used, and it is never shown per person outside the organizer.
- **Weekly during outreach:** count RSVPs by lean; if either side is under 25% a week out, all remaining invitations go to that side.
- **Per-salon record** in SALON_TOPICS.md: date, attendance, split (aggregate), discussants, the decision or non-decision, follow-ups created.
- **Consent:** RSVP emails go to the mailing list only if the box is ticked; nothing about attendees is published; Chatham House rule stated on the RSVP page and at the top of every session.

## 9. Budget and logistics

- **Budget per salon:** ~$250–600 (light food and drink for 20, printing, Luma) — out of pocket until incorporation, tracked for later reimbursement/founding-donor accounting.
- **Room:** anything that seats 24 at a table or hollow square, quiet enough for one conversation; a student-chapter co-host solves this on campus. Not researched further by decision (2026-08-18).
- **Materials:** the brief; the project one-pager; a sign-in sheet that mirrors the RSVP form; name tags with first name and (optional) affiliation only.
- **Follow-up:** thank-you within 48 hours with the recorded decision, next date, and one specific ask per person.

## 10. Risks

- **Capture** — one side's people become the room. Mitigation is the whole plan: matched-pair org outreach, the 25% floor, "bring someone from the other side."
- **Heard as an amendment sales pitch** — especially by academics. Lead with the open question and the published rejections; the discussion has to be able to change the decision, and the record has to show that it did.
- **The privacy topic heard as an abortion topic** — post-*Dobbs*, "right to privacy" carries that freight; the brief must put the informational-vs-autonomy split on the table as the question, and the moderator must keep it there.
- **Faculty and practitioners saying yes and not coming** — confirm discussants individually, send the pre-read, and over-invite by ~40%.
- **Reputational risk to invitees** — some conservatives will not attend a room they suspect is progressive, and vice versa; the host committee's names and the visible presence of the other side are the answer, and the invitation should say who else is coming.
- **Scale creep** — a big public panel is easier to fill and worse for the purpose. Keep the monthly salon small; use co-branded larger events (Zócalo, bar sections, campus) as feeders.

## 11. Metrics (reviewed after salon 3)

- Attendance 14–24 with neither side under 25%.
- ≥ 1 decision changed or confirmed per salon, recorded in SALON_TOPICS.md.
- ≥ 3 substantive review contributions per salon into the research corpus with attribution.
- Repeat rate ≥ 40% by salon 3; ≥ 50% of the room sourced through someone other than Michael by salon 3.
- ≥ 2 volunteers per salon activated on a task within two weeks; ≥ 1 founding-donor conversation per salon.
- ≥ 1 co-hosted or feeder event on the calendar by December.
