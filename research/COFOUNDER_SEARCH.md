# Co-founder / founding-board search

*Drafted 2026-08-19 from Michael's question about how to find a political-nonprofit co-founder. Internal working doc, not public copy. Legal claims marked ⚖️ need verification by counsel or a sourced research pass before being relied on.*

## The reframe

Generic co-founder advice for "political nonprofits" (find campaign veterans, align on ideology, match risk tolerance for political combat) mostly inverts what this project needs:

- **Ideological alignment is the wrong test.** The brand is the full-spectrum test; a co-founder who reads as a veteran of either side's campaigns brands the project on day one. The alignment test is **commitment to the method** — the bipartisan floor, publishing what fails, the veil-of-ignorance drafting rule. The ideal is a visibly cross-spectrum *pair*: Michael plus someone whose public profile balances his, or two senior figures (one from each tradition) as co-chairs of a founding board rather than a single "co-founder."
- **Screen for the opposite of political combat:** people trusted enough by both sides to sit at a Fed Soc + ACS table — exactly the room the salon plan already designs.
- **Startup "co-founder" framing imports an equity-like negotiation the nonprofit form doesn't need.** What a 501(c)(3) legally needs is a board. A balanced founding board of known names does more for credibility (and IRS optics) than one co-founder.

## Structural note — c3, lobbying, and a future c4 arm ⚖️

The site says the plan is a 501(c)(3). One wrinkle counsel must get right: **advocating adoption/ratification of a constitutional amendment counts as lobbying for c3 purposes** — the §4911 regulations' definition of "legislation" includes proposed constitutional amendments and ballot measures. A c3 may do *limited* lobbying (take the 501(h) election; stay within the expenditure caps); everything in the current phase — research, publishing, polling, salons, civic education — is comfortably c3. A full-throttle ratification campaign would eventually need a **c4 arm alongside the c3** (the standard dual structure; American Promise, the closest analogue, runs one). ⚖️ Verify the lobbying-definition point against Treas. Reg. §56.4911-2 before relying on it; verification assigned to the org-landscape research pass (see `research/orgs/`).

Board mechanics ⚖️: California permits a small board, but IRS practice and funders expect **3+ majority-independent directors**; California's public-benefit rule limiting *compensated* persons to at most 49% of the board (Cal. Corp. Code §5227) is mostly moot for an unpaid founding board but should be confirmed by counsel.

## Sequence (recommended)

1. **Host committee first** — already the open item in SALON_OUTREACH_PLAN. 4–6 credible, balanced names; a lower-cost, reversible version of a founding board. The co-founder, if one exists, emerges from it.
2. **Pro bono incorporation counsel** is the most urgent specific person to find (the plan already flags this as the most valuable single contribution). This solves "compliance knowledge we lack" without requiring a co-founder. LA-specific pools: the bar sections and public-law firms in SALON_OUTREACH_TARGETS; law-school clinics (UCLA, USC, Loyola).
3. **Founding board before "co-founder."** Recruit via the ask ladder's top rung; the salon lets us observe candidates engaging with the actual work (do they read a memo and argue with it? do they bring someone from the other side?) before any ask.

## Candidate pools (adapted from the generic playbook)

- **Governance-reform ecosystem, not "political nonprofits":** boards and recently departed senior staff of American Promise, Issue One, Unite America, RepresentUs, Protect Democracy, the Institute for Responsive Government, U.S. Term Limits, state Common Cause chapters. Look up boards via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer (projects.propublica.org/nonprofits) and Candid/GuideStar (candid.org). These people have the network *and* have already chosen cross-partisan reform work. (Also study these orgs as partners/competitors — see `research/orgs/`.)
- **Salon Tier A list** — the existing verified targets are a better pool than any keyword search.
- **Training-program alumni** — usable, but Emily's List and the Leadership Institute are hard-coded partisan: recruit from one only alongside its mirror (the 25%-floor rule, applied to people).
- **Co-founder platforms (CoFoundersLab etc.)** — low priority; the artifact-first path above dominates.

## Vetting questions (method alignment, not ideology)

1. Do they accept the bipartisan floor as a *binding* gate — including on ideas they personally love? (Show them a rejected page they'll hate.)
2. Will their public profile read as cross-spectrum, or will it code the project? Who from the other tradition would vouch for them?
3. Funding philosophy: the decided path is founding donors ($1,000+), no asks at salons, grants later. A candidate who wants to chase major political donors first is a mismatch.
4. Do they understand c3 limits and the eventual c3/c4 structure — or defer to counsel who does?
5. Time and horizon: amendment work is measured in decades; what's their realistic commitment?

## How this slots into existing plans

Rungs 4–5 of the ask ladder (SALON_OUTREACH_PLAN §6) are the recruitment mechanism; the host committee is the proving ground; the founding-donor round seats the founding board. No cold-outreach program needed yet.
