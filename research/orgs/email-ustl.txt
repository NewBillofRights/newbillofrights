Subject: We adopted the U.S. Term Limits amendment word for word — a factual check before we publish

Aaron,

I'm writing to give you notice of something before it's public, and to ask you to correct anything we've gotten wrong.

I run the New Bill of Rights project, a nonpartisan research effort drafting a slate of nine constitutional amendments. Our term limits article opens with U.S. Term Limits' congressional amendment — H.J.Res. 12 and S.J.Res. 1 as introduced in the 119th Congress — adopted word for word, three House terms and two Senate terms included. The page credits U.S. Term Limits by name as the source.

We took your numbers deliberately, and the page says why. Our own research pointed toward twelve-year caps, from California's and Michigan's reforms and from deliberative polling in which majorities of both parties land near twelve years. We chose not to reopen a fight the movement already had in the 1990s. Thirty years of invested effort behind a single text is worth more than our preferred figure, and we state that trade openly on the page rather than burying it.

Three things you should know, so none of this is a surprise:

- We pursue amendments by congressional proposal, not an Article V convention. We take no position on your state application campaign, and our work adds nothing to it.

- Our article adds sections you have no position on: eighteen-year Supreme Court terms, a judicial retirement age, and an anti-evasion clause. The page marks those as ours, not yours.

- Adopting your text is not a claim that you endorse ours. We are not asking to join your campaign or to use your name beyond citation.

Two accuracy questions, which are the real reason I'm writing:

1. We cite fourteen states as having passed the single-subject congressional term limits application, attributed to U.S. Term Limits' own count as of August 2026. Public sources vary. Is fourteen the number you want cited, and as of what date?

2. Is H.J.Res. 12 / S.J.Res. 1 in the 119th Congress the text you consider current?

The site is live but not yet indexed or launched: https://newbillofrights.net/amendments/term-limits

I'm glad to send the draft article as a document if that's easier than a link.
