# The People's Bill of Rights 250, and Katie Fahey

*Research memo, 2026-08-25. Covers a project the [org-landscape pass](README.md) of 2026-08-19
missed: it launched 2026-07-28, three weeks before that pass ran, and nothing in the corpus names
it. Every factual claim below was verified at its source this session; page snapshots are indexed
in [research/cache/index.jsonl](../cache/index.jsonl) under the 2026-08-25 entries. Companion to
[CROSS_CUTTING_ORGS.md](CROSS_CUTTING_ORGS.md), whose Article V analysis is the test this memo
applies.*

## Why this memo exists

Surfaced while checking social handles for our own accounts. The name is close enough to ours to
matter — **"The People's Bill of Rights 250"** against **"New Bill of Rights Foundation"** — and
the subject matter is the same: a nonpartisan-branded effort to put new constitutional amendments
before Congress in 2026. It is the nearest thing to a namesake this project has, and it was
invisible to us until now.

## Headline findings

1. **It is not a competitor for our drafting work, because it has no amendment text.** It is a
   deliberation-and-mandate machine. Text comes later, from lawyers it has not named, out of a
   public process it is still running. We occupy different layers of the same stack: they are
   building public will, we are building drafted language and an evidence record.
2. **They will accept an Article V convention, and say so by name.** This is the finding that
   governs everything else. It puts them on the far side of a recorded founder decision and of the
   ~230-organization anti-convention coalition.
3. **Their proposal set is positive and economic rights** — housing, childcare, prescription
   costs, social security, a living wage. Ours are structural rules that bind officials. These are
   not two versions of one project; they are different theories of what a constitution is for.
4. **The nonpartisan branding is not matched by the coalition or the rhetoric.** The named partners
   and the launch copy code left, in vocabulary our own voice guide bans by name.
5. **The name collision is real, and it is asymmetric in their favor.** They launched publicly with
   celebrity amplification and hold the matching handle on five platforms. We are noindexed.
6. **Katie Fahey is a credible operator with a genuine win**, and she is already in our corpus
   unnamed — [REDISTRICTING_RESEARCH.md](../REDISTRICTING_RESEARCH.md) cites her Michigan campaign.

## 1. The project

**Launched 2026-07-28** (the About page's published date; homepage modified 2026-08-07), pegged to
the 250th anniversary of the Declaration. Branding runs "1776 — 2026" and
"America Turns 250. So Do Our Rights."

**The process, in their words**, is three steps: *Speak Up* (submit rights you think the country
should guarantee), *Show Up* ("small, live, online conversation" to shape ideas), *Step Up*
("the strongest ideas are written into real constitutional amendments," then a national vote).

**The threshold.** From the About FAQ: "Once a strong majority of Americans - for example, 70% or
more - support proposed amendments, the results are presented to Congress and state legislatures
under Article V."

Note the construction "for example, 70% or more." The number is illustrative, not a committed bar.
Compare our own full-spectrum test, which fixes a measured bipartisan floor and publishes what
fails it. **Theirs is a mandate-building exercise; ours is a screening rule.** The difference is
that a screening rule can return "no."

**Two domains.** [peoplesbillofrights250.org](https://peoplesbillofrights250.org/) carries the
explanation; [thepeoplesrights250.org](https://thepeoplesrights250.org/) carries the voting. A
media kit sits at [media.pbor250.org](https://media.pbor250.org/) — built on Lovable, which is a
fair signal of how lightweight the infrastructure is.

## 2. Route — the central finding

Our [CROSS_CUTTING_ORGS.md](CROSS_CUTTING_ORGS.md) records the standing rule: **declare the
congressional-proposal route explicitly**, because the anti-convention coalition opposes the
mechanism rather than the amendments, and the congressional route costs nothing while keeping the
field available. The founder decision is on record.

PBOR250 goes the other way. From their About FAQ, verbatim:

> "This mandate can then trigger one of two pathways to change: either Congress initiates a
> constitutional amendment process, **or a Convention of States is convened**, where
> representatives from every state come together to draft and debate new amendments or sections of
> a modernized Constitution."

Their media kit is the same, in flatter language: "Amendments can be proposed by two-thirds of
Congress or through a convention called by two-thirds of the states. **Either way**, they must then
be ratified by three-fourths of the states."

Three things follow.

- **"Convention of States" is a branded term**, not a neutral description — it is the name of the
  Meckler organization our term-limits memo places on the Article V track. Whether PBOR250 means
  the generic mechanism or the organization, using the phrase reads as an endorsement of the route
  to everyone already fighting about it.
- **"or sections of a modernized Constitution"** goes further than any convention advocate we have
  catalogued. A convention empowered to redraft *sections* of the Constitution is precisely the
  runaway-convention scenario that Common Cause, the Brennan Center, the League of Women Voters,
  the NAACP and the John Birch Society all organize against — from both directions.
- **This alone forecloses coalition** under the recorded decision. Not because their goals are
  objectionable, but because standing next to them imports an argument we deliberately declined.

## 3. The proposal set — a different theory of a constitution

The media kit publishes fourteen "Most Americans Agree" graphics, which is the clearest available
statement of what the campaign is actually pushing:

> Represent People · Full-Time Work · Medical Emergency · Equal Rights · Veterans · Childcare ·
> Constitution · Housing · Social Security · Prescription Costs · Dreamers · Truth · Not Divided ·
> Authorship

The About page adds "a right to healthcare, equality, digital privacy, or environmental
protections."

**Most of these are positive rights — entitlements to a good or service.** Housing, childcare,
prescription costs, social security, full-time work. Our nine amendments are, without exception,
*restraints on official power and rules of procedure*: who may serve how long, who may spend what
in elections, who is bound by law, who may be pardoned, how districts are drawn.

Our [MISSION.md](../MISSION.md) states the principle directly — "Constitutional text should carry
durable principles: rights, limits, and duties that hold for a century. Dollar amounts,
thresholds, procedures, and agency designs belong in legislation." A constitutional right to
childcare is the category that principle excludes.

This is not a criticism to make publicly. It is the reason the two projects are not substitutes and
should never be described as versions of one idea. **We should be able to state the distinction in
one sentence if asked, without disparaging theirs.** Something close to: *they are asking what the
country should guarantee people; we are asking what rules should bind the people in power.*

Note also what our own registry already holds: `privacy-data-protection` is a registered candidate
clause, and digital privacy appears on their list too. That is the one genuine overlap, and it sits
in our candidate bucket, not our proposed nine.

## 4. Who is actually behind it

**Operators** (About page): "powered by Fame Currency, The People, Frankly, and Good Creator Group."

**Partner logos** (media kit): Change.org · Frankly · People First · The People · Fame Currency.

**Coalition and champions** ([Ms. Magazine, 2026-07-29](https://msmagazine.com/2026/07/29/peoples-bill-of-rights-era-equal-rights-amendment-alicia-keys-constitution/)):
the ERA Coalition ("300+ organizations"), Fame Currency (founded by Leigh Blake), She Should Run,
and RepresentWomen. **Alicia Keys** is named "one of the initiative's most visible champions."
Named individuals: Katie Fahey; Leigh Blake; Tamaya Dennard, RepresentWomen.

**The People** ([thepeople.org](https://www.thepeople.org/home)) is a **registered 501(c)(3)** in
Grand Rapids, Michigan (2153 Wealthy Street SE #194). Programs: the Respect Voters Coalition
(ballot-initiative defense), Operation Vote America, and **DELIBERATIONS.US**, which runs
nonpartisan deliberation in schools and communities.

⚠️ **A structural observation worth carrying.** Our cross-cutting memo verified at the regulation
that **§56.4911-2(d)(1)(i) names "constitutional amendment" by name** — amendment advocacy is
lobbying for 501(c)(3) purposes. The People appears to be c3-only, with no c4 partner disclosed,
while fronting a national constitutional-amendment campaign. Either they are relying on the
nonpartisan-analysis exception (which our memo found covers a research phase but not work "timed or
channeled" to a vote), or they have a c4 they have not published. **This validates our own
decision** — 501(h) election at formation, c3/c4 pair on the American Promise model — and it is a
live exposure for them.

## 5. Katie Fahey

Born June 4, 1989 in Livonia, Michigan; Aquinas College 2011, degrees in Sustainable Business and
Community Leadership. Early career at SpartanNash and the Michigan Recycling Coalition.

**Voters Not Politicians (2016–2019)** is the record that matters. After a Facebook post asking who
wanted to take on gerrymandering, she built a volunteer campaign that held 33 town halls, gathered
**over 425,000 signatures from all 83 Michigan counties**, and passed **Proposal 2 with 61% on
November 6, 2018**, creating a 13-member independent redistricting commission with randomly
selected, party-balanced members — surviving legal challenge funded by the Michigan Chamber of
Commerce. She is the subject of *Slay the Dragon* (Tribeca, 2019).

**Executive Director of The People since March 2019.** Wikipedia records her as **a registered
independent with no party affiliation** — which is worth knowing, because it is a genuine
nonpartisan credential that the campaign's coalition does not match.

**She is already in our corpus, unnamed.** [REDISTRICTING_RESEARCH.md:91](../REDISTRICTING_RESEARCH.md)
cites Michigan Proposal 2 and Voters Not Politicians as a model — "a pure volunteer petition drive;
13 citizens (4 R, 4 D, 5 unaffiliated) selected by mailed lottery." Her method — 33 town halls,
citizen deliberation, then drafted text — is *structurally the same method* as our
research → polling → salon → draft sequence. **She is the closest thing in American politics to a
proof of concept for our own process**, at state level.

## 6. Partisan coding

The branding says "100% POLITICIAN, SPECIAL INTERESTS & PARTISAN-FREE," "Not Red. Not Blue. Just
You," and "guided by constitutional scholars, experts on both sides of an issue." The execution
does not hold that line.

- **The named coalition is one-sided.** ERA Coalition, She Should Run, RepresentWomen, Alicia Keys.
  Every named partner sits in the same half of the spectrum. No conservative organization is named.
- **The rhetoric codes.** From the About page: "**THE ELITES ARE PLAYING WITH OUR RIGHTS / IT'S TIME
  WE TOOK THEM BACK**." From the homepage: "For 250 years, the Constitution has shaped who holds
  power, and who is kept at the margins," and "making sure those who were left out when the
  Constitution was first written, now have a real voice."
- **Our [COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md](../../COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md) bans "elites" by name**, alongside
  "populist," "establishment," "radical." The word is on our prohibited list precisely because it
  reads as a side.
- **The proposal set codes.** Dreamers, prescription costs, childcare and a living wage are not
  neutral selections, whatever their polling.

The honest summary: **cross-partisan in stated intent, single-coalition in execution.** That is the
failure mode our own mission's full-spectrum test exists to prevent, and it is the most useful
thing this project teaches us — a nonpartisan label does not survive a one-sided partner list.

## 7. The name collision

| | New Bill of Rights Foundation | The People's Bill of Rights 250 |
|---|---|---|
| Public since | not yet (noindexed) | 2026-07-28 |
| Amendment text | nine drafted articles, published | none yet |
| Route | congressional proposal only | congressional **or** convention |
| Rights theory | structural limits on power | positive and economic rights |
| Method | research → polling → salon → draft | crowdsource → deliberate → vote |
| Failure published | yes — `/rejected`, 16 entries | not offered |
| Coalition | none yet, by design | ERA Coalition, She Should Run, RepresentWomen |
| Reach | pre-launch | Alicia Keys; five platforms; Change.org |

**The exposure.** At launch we will be the second "bill of rights" amendment project of 2026, and
the one with no celebrity, no coalition and no press. Search results for "new bill of rights" will
compete against a campaign holding @peoplesbillofrights250 on Instagram, TikTok, Threads, Bluesky
and Facebook. Some fraction of readers will assume we are a spinoff, a rival faction, or the same
thing.

**What actually protects us**, in order of strength:

1. **We have text and an evidence record; they have a process.** Nine drafted articles with 698
   footnotes, a published rejected bucket, and named source organizations is not a thing that can
   be confused with a crowdsourcing campaign for long by anyone who reads either.
2. **The route difference is a clean, stateable line.** "We do not seek a convention" separates us
   from them and from Convention of States in one sentence, and it is already a recorded decision.
3. **The visual identity is genuinely distinct** — navy and gold institutional seal against their
   yellow-and-black campaign look.
4. **"Foundation" in the name does real work here.** It reads as an institution rather than a
   campaign, which is the distinction we actually want.

**What I would not do:** rename. The name predates theirs in our own work, the domain is held, the
identity is built, and their campaign is pegged to an anniversary that has already passed — a
250th-anniversary hook has a natural decay that our project does not share.

## 8. Partnership and conflict

- **Not a coalition partner.** The convention position alone forecloses it under the recorded
  decision, and the one-sided partner list would code us on arrival. This needs no hostility; it is
  the same "cite, do not join" posture the term-limits memo takes toward Fix the Court.
- **Not an adversary either.** They hold no competing text, attack nothing we propose, and occupy a
  different layer. Nothing about them requires a response from us.
- **DELIBERATIONS.US is worth studying before our salon program runs.** They are doing structured
  citizen deliberation at scale, which is the thing [SALON_OUTREACH_PLAN.md](../SALON_OUTREACH_PLAN.md)
  proposes to do in a room. Their published method is a free comparator.
- **Fahey personally: keep citing Michigan, seek nothing.** Proposal 2 is already load-bearing in
  our redistricting research and should stay. An approach to her would be premature and would put
  the convention question on the table before we have launched.
- **Watch item.** If PBOR250 produces actual amendment text, re-run the language comparison — that
  is the moment it becomes a drafting question rather than a landscape one.
