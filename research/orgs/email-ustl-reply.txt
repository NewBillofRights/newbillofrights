Subject: Re: We adopted the U.S. Term Limits amendment word for word — a factual check before we publish

Aaron,

Thank you — both answers are what I needed. The Arizona date in particular lets us anchor the count rather than citing it loosely, and we'll attribute it to you and date it.

One clarification, because I would rather correct it now than let it stand.

We have adopted the text of your amendment. We have not endorsed the resolution, and the difference matters for how each of us describes this.

What we did: H.J.Res. 12 and S.J.Res. 1 appear word for word as the first three sections of our term limits article, credited to U.S. Term Limits by name on the page. That is a drafting decision — we concluded your language was better than writing our own, and that the movement's thirty years behind a single text was worth more than our own preferred figure.

What we did not do: take a position on the Article V convention campaign, or ask Congress to pass your resolution as a standalone vehicle. Our article is one of nine in a slate, and it continues past your three sections into eighteen-year Supreme Court terms, a judicial retirement age, and an anti-evasion clause — provisions U.S. Term Limits has no position on and should not be associated with. We pursue amendments by congressional proposal rather than by convention. That is a choice about our own project, not a criticism of yours.

I raise it only so your materials and ours describe the relationship the same way. If it helps, I am glad to send you the exact sentences our page uses to credit U.S. Term Limits, so you can tell me whether the characterization is fair before it goes public. I would rather hear an objection from you now than have you read it cold.

Thank you for the document on the reasoning behind three and two. It is a better statement of the case than my summary of it, and the "divide and fizzle" history matches what our own research found independently — that four competing versions reached the floor on March 29, 1995, that the most permissive drew the top vote at 227-204, and that the split is what sank it. That history is a large part of why we took your numbers rather than our own.

One question about the McLaughlin figures in it, and I ask only because we cite every poll with its pollster, fieldwork dates and party breakdown, so I went looking for the wave.

The toplines match your January 22-27, 2025 national survey exactly — summing the "how many 2-year terms" question, 6 years or fewer comes to 66.8% against your 67%, and the 12-year figure to 2.9% against your 3%. Two of the party splits reconcile as well: Republicans at 70.1% and independents at 69.1%.

Two do not. That crosstab puts Democrats favoring 6 years or fewer at 60.2%, where the document says 63%. And at 12 years it shows Republicans 4.6%, Democrats 1.7% and independents 2.2%, where the document says 4%, 3% and 4%. Excluding don't-knows doesn't close the gap either.

So: is the document drawing on a wave you haven't posted publicly? If so I'd be glad to cite that one. If it's the January 2025 wave, you may want to know that two of the party numbers in a handout going to congressional offices don't match your own crosstabs — I'd rather flag it to you than have a member's staff find it.
