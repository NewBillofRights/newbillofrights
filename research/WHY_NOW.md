# Why Now — The Political Timing of the Project

*Research memo, 2026-08-24. Source document for the public timing piece (`/why-now`) and the one-pager's "Why now" section. Drafted at the founder's direction (2026-08-24): make the case that the present moment may be open to structural constitutional reform — in the house voice, which means without a regime narrative. Facts and URLs below come from the existing corpus (POLLING_RESEARCH.md, CIVIL_SERVICE_CANDIDATE.md, PARDON_SELF_PARDON_CLAUSE.md, the amendment pages' footnotes, RATIFICATION_EFFECTS_RESEARCH.md, TERM_LIMITS_ORGS.md) plus two Pew pages fetched and cached 2026-08-24 (`research/cache/index.jsonl`).*

## 1. The framing problem, and how this memo resolves it

The founder's stated premise is that the country is coming off a turbulent stretch of executive-branch conflict and that the climate may be opening. The house rules (COPY_VOICE_GUIDE.md) do not permit that premise to be published as written: no current or recent officeholder is named in prose, no example may implicate one party alone, and the corpus's own method treats numbers that move with the party of the president — "regime-coded" — as disqualifying evidence, not persuasive evidence.

The resolution is not to soften the claim but to relocate it. The publishable timing case rests on three kinds of evidence, each stronger than a regime narrative:

1. **The appetite for structural change is directly measured, and it is enormous** (§2).
2. **Both parties now have recent, symmetric experience of fearing the presidency in the other party's hands** — which the corpus can show in the data itself, without naming anyone: the same questions, asked across a change of administration, collapse and recover in mirror image (§3). This is the veil-of-ignorance argument made concrete: for the first time in the modern polling record, *both* sides have a fresh memory of being on the losing end of executive power.
3. **The movement is already legislative, not just attitudinal** (§4).

And the case carries its own caution (§5): part of the current movement in opinion is regime-coded, the corpus flags it as such, and the project builds only on the numbers that held across administrations. Saying so publicly is not a concession — it is the brand.

## 2. The appetite, quantified

- **77% of Americans say the nation's political system needs major changes or complete reform** (Pew Research Center, Spring 2025 25-country survey, U.S. sample fielded March 24–30, 2025). Pew's four-way segmentation: **49% are "pessimistic reformers"** (want major change, not confident it can happen), **27% optimistic reformers** (want it and believe it can happen), **23% status-quo supporters**. Pew publishes no party breakdown for these climate items. — [Pew, April 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/15/americans-stand-out-internationally-for-their-pessimism-about-the-nations-political-system/); [Pew, July 2026 roundup](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/28/most-americans-favor-big-changes-to-the-political-system-that-likely-would-require-amending-the-constitution/)
- **The U.S. is an outlier**: it has the highest GDP per capita of the 25 countries surveyed but reports reform sentiment at the level of middle-income countries; it is also the only surveyed country where more adults describe the morality and ethics of their fellow citizens as bad than good. — [Pew, April 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/15/americans-stand-out-internationally-for-their-pessimism-about-the-nations-political-system/)
- **Pew itself framed the reform appetite as constitutional**: the July 2026 roundup is titled "Most Americans favor big changes to the political system that likely would require amending the Constitution," and its reform-by-reform numbers carry party splits that match the corpus: congressional term limits 87% (90% R / 86% D, 2023), age limits for federal officials 79% (82% R / 76% D, 2023), Supreme Court age limits 74% (68% R / 82% D, 2023), campaign spending limits 72% (71% R / 76% D, 2023). — [Pew, July 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/28/most-americans-favor-big-changes-to-the-political-system-that-likely-would-require-amending-the-constitution/)
- The gap between the 77% and the 27% is the project's audience in a single statistic: **half the country wants what the project proposes and does not believe it can happen.** The site's answer to the disbelief is the evidence itself — the floors, published with party breakdowns.

## 3. The symmetric experience: the veil of ignorance is now concrete

The corpus's clearest demonstrations that both parties have recently feared the presidency — stated entirely in data, naming no one:

- **Civil service.** The Partnership for Public Service / Impact Research series asked the identical question — whether a nonpartisan civil service is important for a strong American democracy — in three consecutive springs: **87% (2024) → 66% (2025) → 76% (2026) overall; Republicans ~87% → 66% → 78%.** As CIVIL_SERVICE_CANDIDATE.md puts it: "A 21-point fall and a 12-point recovery, on a principle, in 24 months, with no intervening event but the exercise of the power and the reaction to it." — [2024 report](https://ourpublicservice.org/publications/the-state-of-public-trust-in-government-2024/); [2025 report](https://ourpublicservice.org/publications/the-state-of-public-trust-in-government-2025/); [2026 report](https://ourpublicservice.org/know-the-facts/resource-library/reports/steady-opposition-public-disapproval-of-trump-administration-funding-and-workforce-cuts-remains-strong)
- **Pardons.** Each party's voters have, within the last two years, disapproved of the other party's pardons — and each party's floor collapsed on exactly the provision its own president had just used: asked in early 2025 whether pardons to a president's family members are acceptable, 72% of Republicans and 42% of Democrats said unacceptable ([YouGov, Jan.–Feb. 2025](https://ygo-assets-websites-editorial-emea.yougov.net/documents/Acceptability_of_President_Actions_poll_results.pdf); [write-up](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/51548-where-americans-draw-the-line-on-presidential-behavior)). POLLING_RESEARCH.md records pardon limits as the fastest-moving opinion of any category (2024→2026), moved by both parties' controversial pardons.
- **Redistricting.** When Marquette asked about mid-decade redraws in a question naming both Texas and California, opposition was 71% (70% R / 73% D) — and between the September and November 2025 waves, as the second state joined, Republican opposition rose eight points while Democratic opposition fell seven. Symmetric grievance, measured in real time. — [Marquette, Nov. 2025](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MLSPSC29PressReleaseNationalIssues.pdf)
- **The publishable sentence this evidence supports:** *Over the past decade each party has held the presidency, and each has watched powers it once defended used in ways it opposed. That experience is visible in the polling itself — and it is why rules written behind a veil of ignorance, binding all future presidents equally, can now be evaluated by both sides with the same fear in mind.*

## 4. The movement is already legislative

- **Money in politics:** Oklahoma and Idaho became the 24th and 25th states to call on Congress to propose an amendment in spring 2026; Missouri's House passed its resolution unanimously. — [Democracy Renovator](https://www.democracyrenovator.com/p/25-states-back-amendment-to-limit); [The Beacon, Feb. 2026](https://thebeaconnews.org/stories/2026/02/26/missouri-bill-to-overturn-citizens-united/). In June 2026 the For Our Freedom Amendment got its first Republican-introduced congressional version, H.J.Res. 191. — [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-joint-resolution/191/text)
- **Term limits:** U.S. Term Limits reports fourteen states have passed its Article V application (advocacy-sourced count; the fourteenth, Arizona, passed HCR2043 in April 2026), and the amendment text sits in Congress as H.J.Res. 12 / S.J.Res. 1. — [USTL progress page](https://termlimits.com/progress/); [Congress.gov, H.J.Res. 12](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-joint-resolution/12/text); Arizona corroboration in OUTREACH_USTL_AMERICANPROMISE.md ([LegiScan roll call](https://legiscan.com/AZ/rollcall/HCR2043/id/1686579))
- **Congress is acting at the edges of the ethics agenda:** the House passed a ban on foreign contributions to ballot-measure campaigns by voice vote in July 2026 ([Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/07/18/u-s-house-passes-bill-banning-foreign-contributions-to-state-and-local-ballot-measure-campaigns/)) and passed a stock-trading restriction the same month — though that vote was 232–198 and mostly party-line, which the corpus records as a caution, not a proof point ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/07/22/congressional-stock-trading-bill-passes-the-house/)).
- **The precedent for ratification is on point:** the most recent amendment actually ratified — the Twenty-seventh, in 1992 — is an anti-self-dealing rule for Congress, the same species as the project's ethics article. — [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/amendments-11-27); ETHICS_RESEARCH.md §"27th Amendment"

## 5. The caution the piece must carry

The corpus's own findings forbid selling the whole movement as durable:

- PARDON_SELF_PARDON_CLAUSE.md: "a party's support for a limit on the presidency has, in this corpus, moved with which party holds the presidency" — both self-pardon readings sit in alignments that could produce their numbers.
- CIVIL_SERVICE_CANDIDATE.md: "Neither [the 66% nor the 78%] is the durable number."
- The durable why-now numbers are the ones that held across administrations: congressional term limits (90R/86D), the ethics stock ban (87R/88D), campaign spending limits (71R/76D), the symmetric redistricting question (70R/73D), lobbying waits (72R/72D).

The public piece therefore says, in the house voice: *some of today's reform appetite will fade with the next change of administration — we measure for exactly that, and build only on what holds.* This converts the discipline into the argument: the moment is open precisely because so many people want change and so few believe it can happen; a project that shows, with evidence, which changes the whole spectrum already supports is what a pessimistic reformer would need to see.

## 6. What the public piece must never do

No officeholder named. No party's abuses cited without the other's in the same sentence. No "the last two years," "this administration," "coming off," or any dating that a reader would resolve to one presidency. No prediction of ratification ("we" appears only for the foundation's decisions and measurements). The 77% climate number is presented with pollster and year and the express note that Pew publishes no party split for it; every reform-specific number carries its split.
