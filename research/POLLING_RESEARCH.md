# Polling Research & Citations

Public survey evidence on bipartisan support for reform ideas. Gathered 2026-08-06 (initial pass and in-depth pass). Organized to match [CATEGORIES.md](CATEGORIES.md). The bar we care about: strong majorities in **both** parties, not just a high topline.

**How to read the summary metric.** For each category we take the best-polled core proposal and report the **bipartisan floor** — the *lower* of Republican and Democratic support. The floor is the honest number: a 75% topline with a 40% floor is a partisan idea with a good press release. Verdicts: **Strong** = floor ≥ 60% · **Moderate** = floor 45–59% · **Weak** = floor < 45% · **Unpolled** = no credible national data.

Advocacy-commissioned polls are flagged throughout; independent pollsters (Pew, Gallup, Marquette, AP-NORC, YouGov/Economist, Fox News, Monmouth) are weighted more heavily in the verdicts.

## Target Category Detail

### 1. Campaign Finance / Money in Politics

- Limits on individual/organizational campaign spending: **72% overall (71% R / 76% D)** — [Pew Research, July 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/23/7-facts-about-americans-views-of-money-in-politics/); only 11% oppose; re-cited in [Pew's July 2026 roundup](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/28/most-americans-favor-big-changes-to-the-political-system-that-likely-would-require-amending-the-constitution/).
- Constitutional amendment to overturn Citizens United-line rulings: **79% (75% R / 84% D / 81% I)** — [Brennan Center, June 2026](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/poll-voters-want-solutions-government-corruption) (advocacy-adjacent); also covered by [OpenSecrets](https://www.opensecrets.org/news/2026/06/new-poll-shows-massive-cross-party-support-for-ending-dark-money/).
- Dark-money donor disclosure: **85% (85% R / 88% D / 84% I)** — [Brennan Center, June 2026](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/poll-voters-want-solutions-government-corruption).
- "For Our Freedom" amendment (regulate/limit money in elections): **71% (72% R / 76% D / 74% I)** — [Ipsos for American Promise, July 2025](https://americanpromise.net/americans-agree-protect-elections-with-a-constitutional-amendment/) (advocacy-commissioned, Ipsos probability panel); 82% call money in politics a threat to elections (80% R / 91% D).
- Overturn Citizens United (trend anchor): **75% (66% R / 85% D)** — [UMD via Center for Public Integrity, May 2018](https://publicintegrity.org/politics/study-most-americans-want-to-kill-citizens-united-with-constitutional-amendment/); [Time coverage](https://time.com/5272988/citizens-united-campaign-finance-constitution/). The 19-pt gap has narrowed since.
- Ban foreign funding of ballot initiatives: **80% (77% R / 84% D / 74% I)** — [UMD PPC, 2021](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/foreign-funding-of-ballot-initiatives/); House passed a ban by voice vote [July 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/07/18/u-s-house-passes-bill-banning-foreign-contributions-to-state-and-local-ballot-measure-campaigns/).
- Limit election spending by U.S. corporations with foreign ownership: **82%** — [Data for Progress, July 2024](https://www.dataforprogress.org/blog/2024/7/10/voters-want-to-ban-foreign-corporations-from-influencing-us-elections) (progressive advocacy pollster).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Public financing is the weak remedy: only ~50% support — [Gallup, 2013](https://news.gallup.com/poll/163208/half-support-publicly-financed-federal-campaigns.aspx). Limits and disclosure are the consensus remedies.
- Feasibility cynicism: just 59% think laws could effectively reduce money's role (52% R) — [Pew 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/23/7-facts-about-americans-views-of-money-in-politics/).
- Shared diagnosis is near-universal: 85% (85% R / 85% D) say campaign costs deter good candidates; 80% say donors have too much influence (Pew 2023).
- Advocacy polls run ~5–10 pts hotter (79–85%) than independent ones (70–75%) — both are supermajorities.

### 2. Term Limits and Ethics Rules for Supreme Court & Federal Judiciary

- Fixed terms instead of life tenure: **79% (73% R / 87% D / 71% I)** — [Marquette Law School Poll, May 2026](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/06/04/detailed-results-of-the-marquette-law-school-supreme-court-poll-may-20-26-2026-court-issues/); Marquette consistently finds low-to-high 70s.
- Set number of years rather than life terms: **67% (57% R / 82% D)** — [AP-NORC, July 2022](https://apnorc.org/ap-norc-poll-2-in-3-in-us-favor-term-limits-for-justices/); the 25-pt gap has narrowed since.
- 18-year term limit: **78%** (up from 66% in 2022; R support +17 pts) — [Fox News, July 2024](https://www.foxnews.com/official-polls/fox-news-poll-supreme-court-approval-rating-drops-record-low); same poll: 81% favor a mandatory retirement age.
- Term limits for justices: **70% (56% R / 89% D)** — [YouGov, July 2024](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/50159-most-americans-support-term-limits-poll) — widest gap measured (33 pts).
- Term limits: **75%** (PRRI 2025); **74%, net +59** (Navigator, May 2026 — Democratic-aligned, flag) — via [Fix the Court](https://fixthecourt.com/2026/05/scotus-reforms-from-term-limits-to-ethics-enforcement-remain-popular/) (advocacy) and [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/supreme-court-term-limits-democrat-major-reform-change-12018759).
- Max age limit for justices: **74% (68% R / 82% D)** — [Pew, July 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/28/most-americans-favor-big-changes-to-the-political-system-that-likely-would-require-amending-the-constitution/).
- Enforcing a strict ethics code for justices: **90% (88% R / 93% D)** — [Marquette, Oct 2024](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2024/10/17/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-approval-of-u-s-supreme-court-edges-upward-but-55-disapprove-of-the-courts-work/) — the closest thing to consensus in this category.
- Binding ethics code: **70% (62% R / 86% D)** — [YouGov, July 2024](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/50159-most-americans-support-term-limits-poll).
- Extend stock-trading ban to President, VP, and justices: **87% (87% R / 90% D / 82% I)** — [UMD PPC, May 2023](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/stock-trading-by-members-of-congress/).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Wording drives the R number: "fixed terms" (Marquette) → 73% R; "term limits" as a reform package (YouGov/AP-NORC) → 56–57% R. "Enforcing a strict ethics code" (88% R) far outpolls "binding code of ethics" (62% R).
- Clear upward trend 2022→2026, driven mostly by Republicans (Fox 66→78; AP-NORC 67 → Marquette 79).
- Sharp contrast case: court expansion polls ~40% with a 41-pt gap — validates our rejection.
- Polls rarely mention an amendment may be required, which typically depresses support.
- Recusal/financial-disclosure rules for lower-court judges are essentially unpolled; closest data is the UMD stock-ban extension (87%).

### 3. Term Limits and Ethics Rules for Congress

- Congressional term limits: **87% (90% R / 86% D)** — [Pew, July 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/28/most-americans-favor-big-changes-to-the-political-system-that-likely-would-require-amending-the-constitution/) — Pew's most-supported structural reform; 4-pt gap.
- Term-limits amendment (deliberative format): **83% (86% R / 80% D / 84% I)** — [UMD PPC, Feb 2023](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/congressional-term-limits/); majorities chose 4 House / 2 Senate terms as the caps.
- Term limits: **83% (85% R / 79% D / 85% I)** — [McLaughlin for U.S. Term Limits, Jan 2025](https://termlimits.com/new-poll-83-of-americans-support-term-limits-for-congress/) ([PDF](https://termlimits.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Mclaughlin_National_Poll_2025.pdf)) — advocacy-commissioned but matches UMD exactly.
- Historical anchor: **75% (82% R / 65% D / 79% I)** — [Gallup, Jan 2013](https://news.gallup.com/poll/159881/americans-call-term-limits-end-electoral-college.aspx); D support has since climbed ~15–20 pts.
- Max age limit for elected officials: **79% (82% R / 76% D)** — [Pew, July 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/28/most-americans-favor-big-changes-to-the-political-system-that-likely-would-require-amending-the-constitution/).
- Stock-trading ban: **86% (87% R / 88% D / 81% I)** — [UMD PPC, May 2023](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/stock-trading-by-members-of-congress/); 92% found the conflict-of-interest argument convincing.
- Stock-trading ban (quick poll): **63% (58% R / 69% D / 64% I)** — [Morning Consult, Jan 2022](https://intel.morningconsult.com/mc-content/instant-intel/ban-stock-trading-congress-poll).
- "Not allowed to trade individual stocks": **76%** (vs. 71% when called a "ban") — [Economist/YouGov, May 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54851-most-americans-think-congressional-corruption-is-widespread-may-22-26-2026-economist-yougov-poll) — wording experiment; same poll found most Americans think congressional corruption is widespread.
- Stock-trading ban legislation: **~80%** — [Stand Up America, Sept 2022](https://standupamerica.com/2022/09/new-stand-up-america-poll-nearly-80-of-americans-support-legislation-to-ban-congressional-stock-trading/) (progressive advocacy; no party split).
- Momentum: the House passed a stock-trading restriction bill in July 2026 — [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/07/22/congressional-stock-trading-bill-passes-the-house/), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/22/politics/stock-trading-restriction-congress).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Smallest partisan gap of any reform researched (3–7 pts) and highest toplines (83–87%) — but polls omit that *U.S. Term Limits v. Thornton* (1995) means an Article V amendment is required. UMD PPC support held at 83% even after counterarguments.
- Stock-ban support is format-sensitive: ~63% in quick polls, 86–87% after deliberation; the word "ban" costs ~5 pts.
- Extension cuts both ways: extending the trading ban up to President/VP/SCOTUS holds at 87%, but extending to all federal employees craters to **40%** (UMD PPC) — the public targets elected/appointed power, not the civil service.

### 4. Ethics Rules for President

- Ban presidential financial conflicts of interest: **83% overall (no party split published)** — [Brennan Center, April–May 2026](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/poll-voters-want-solutions-government-corruption) (advocacy-adjacent).
- Candidates should release tax returns: **67% (30% R / 91% D)** on Trump-specific framing — [Quinnipiac, March 2018](https://thehill.com/policy/finance/374875-poll-two-thirds-of-voters-think-trump-should-release-his-tax-returns/); generic framing: 67% (60% R) — [Morning Consult via Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Fact_check/Is_it_true_that_most_people_don't_care_whether_Republican_presidential_nominee_Donald_Trump_releases_his_tax_returns); 68% say voters have a right to see candidates' financial records (Reuters/Ipsos 2016).
- Trump must "choose between being president and businessman": **67%** — but **69% said forcing a sale of businesses "goes too far"** — [Bloomberg, Dec 2016](https://www.npr.org/2016/12/09/504889540/poll-trump-needs-to-choose-between-presidency-and-his-business). Abstract concern high; mandated divestment polls far worse.
- Qatar jet "raises ethical concerns about corruption": **62% (40% R / 85% D / 62% I)** — [Harvard CAPS/Harris, May 2025](https://harvardharrispoll.com/category/2017-01-national-poll/may-2025/) ([Statista chart](https://www.statista.com/chart/34498/us-views-on-trumps-acceptance-of-qatar-plane/)); "Qatar is trying to bribe Trump": 54% — [Yahoo/YouGov, May 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/poll-most-americans-think-qatar-is-trying-to-bribe-trump-with-luxury-jet-gift-185933774.html); jet a "bad idea": 51%; business interests affect his decisions: 73% — [Economist/YouGov, May 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52223-federal-budget-immigration-donald-trump-businesses-may-16-19-2025-economist-yougov-poll).
- Bar officials/families from profiting from crypto in office: **71% (67% R)**; still 66% (55% R) after counterargument — [Progressive Policy Institute, 2025](https://www.progressivepolicy.org/as-trump-pockets-1-2-billion-from-crypto-new-poll-finds-voters-want-the-industry-and-self-dealing-officials-reined-in/) (center-left advocacy).
- Oppose broad presidential immunity: **61% oppose (61% R favor / 17% D favor)** — [Marquette, Feb 2025](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2025/02/13/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-approval-of-u-s-supreme-court-above-50-for-first-time-since-march-2022/); only **20% agree** presidents should have blanket immunity — [Economist/YouGov, June 2024](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/50017-supreme-court-2024-rulings-what-americans-think-poll). Navigator (D-aligned) similar — [July 2024](https://navigatorresearch.org/most-americans-oppose-the-supreme-court-presidential-immunity-decision/).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Framing is decisive: two-thirds see the problem, but a forced-divestment mandate flipped to 69% "goes too far." Modern party-split polling on blind trusts/divestment is scarce.
- Immunity wording moves Republicans dramatically (R support roughly doubles when Trump is named vs. "former presidents" generically).
- Emoluments enforcement per se is essentially unpolled; foreign-gift questions are the proxy and show 40–60-pt gaps.
- The bipartisan openings here: crypto/self-dealing bans (67% R even before deliberation) and generic financial-disclosure principles (60% R). Person-coded framings are poison.

### 5. Privacy

- More government regulation of what companies do with personal data: **72% (68% R / 78% D)**, only 7% want less — [Pew, May 2023 (n=5,101)](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/10/18/how-americans-view-data-privacy/) — rare single-digit-gap issue.
- Congress should prioritize federal data-protection legislation: **~83% (81% R / 86% D)** — [Morning Consult, 2023](https://pro.morningconsult.com/instant-intel/state-privacy-congress-priority-poll).
- A specific federal privacy-bill mechanism: **56% (54% R / 62% D / 50% I)** — [Politico/Morning Consult, Jan 2022](https://intel.morningconsult.com/mc-content/instant-intel/federal-data-privacy-legislation-polling) — "protect my data" polls far better than any concrete bill.
- Warrant before government *buys* location/internet data from brokers: **80%**; warrant before searching Americans' communications (§702): **76%** — [YouGov for Demand Progress, Dec 2023](https://demandprogresseducationfund.org/new-polling-as-mass-surveillance-debate-reaches-final-stages-in-congress-americans-demonstrate-overwhelming-support-for-increased-privacy-protections/) (advocacy-commissioned; no party splits published).
- Concern about government use of personal data: **71%, up from 64% in 2019 (R 63→77%)** — [Pew, May 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/10/18/views-of-data-privacy-risks-personal-data-and-digital-privacy-laws/).
- Broad government data collection is an "unnecessary intrusion": **56%**; concern surveillance could target political opponents: **71%** — [YouGov, June 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52425-what-americans-think-about-privacy-united-states-government-surveillance-in-2025-poll).
- Constitutional right to privacy: **>60%** — [DuckDuckGo survey, Dec 2018](https://spreadprivacy.com/privacy-legislation-survey/) (advocacy; no reputable equivalent with party splits exists — a real polling gap).
- Caution: police facial recognition polls *positively* — 46% good vs. 27% bad (48% R / 43% D) — [Pew, Nov 2021](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2022/03/17/public-more-likely-to-see-facial-recognition-use-by-police-as-good-rather-than-bad-for-society/).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Most bipartisan framing: consumer data privacy (5–12-pt gaps). Government-surveillance framings repolarize by who holds power (R majorities in 2025 found monitoring journalists/activists acceptable; Democrats did not — and the pattern flips with administrations).
- Support varies with specificity: "protect data" ~80%; a concrete bill ~56%.
- A "constitutional right to privacy" amendment is effectively unpolled by gold-standard pollsters — worth commissioning; note abortion-adjacency risk in how respondents may hear "right to privacy."

### Rejected 2026-08-06: Eliminate Qualified Immunity for Policing

- Eliminate QI so police can be sued: **63% (42% R / 79% D / 64% I)** — [Cato/YouGov, July 2020](https://www.cato.org/survey-reports/poll-63-americans-favor-eliminating-qualified-immunity-police); 58% of Republicans *oppose*.
- Civilians need the power to sue police for misconduct: **66% (45% R / 84% D)** — [Pew, June 2020 (n=4,708)](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2020/07/09/majority-of-public-favors-giving-civilians-the-power-to-sue-police-officers-for-misconduct/).
- End QI: **59% (46% R / 73% D / 59% I)** — [Vox/Data for Progress, April 2021, via Reason](https://reason.com/2021/06/07/can-republicans-be-persuaded-to-restrict-qualified-immunity/); as part of the George Floyd Justice in Policing Act: 56% (39% R / 73% D) — [Data for Progress, March 2021](https://www.dataforprogress.org/blog/2021/3/12/voters-support-justice-in-policing-act) (progressive pollster).
- UMD PPC deliberative (July 2020): ~62% (40% R), though 61% of R rated it at least "tolerable" — [via Reason](https://reason.com/2021/06/07/can-republicans-be-persuaded-to-restrict-qualified-immunity/).
- KEY REFRAME: officers should be "held accountable for misconduct, even if unaware their actions were illegal": **84% (64% R / 91% D / 75% I)** — [Cato/YouGov 2020](https://www.cato.org/survey-reports/poll-63-americans-favor-eliminating-qualified-immunity-police). Accountability framing wins Republicans; "eliminate QI"/"sue police" loses them.
- Academic retest (Oct 2020, pub. Jan 2026): ~45/46 split on keeping QI; support for suits highly scenario-dependent — [*Policing* (Oxford)](https://academic.oup.com/policing/article/doi/10.1093/police/paag021/8688018) (MTurk convenience sample).

#### Nuances & caveats

- **Least bipartisan target category.** R support for elimination is 39–46% in every credible poll; gaps run 34–39 pts.
- All headline numbers are from the post-George Floyd window (2020–21). UMass Amherst found support already sliding by [April 2021](https://www.umass.edu/news/article/new-national-umass-amherst-poll-finds-decreasing-support-police-reform-black-lives) (59%→54%), and **no major national poll has re-asked since** — current support is likely lower than every number above.
- Strategic implication: as framed ("eliminate qualified immunity"), this fails the full-spectrum test on current evidence. The accountability framing (84%, 64% R) is the only version that clears the bar. **The category was rejected 2026-08-06 on this evidence**; it can return if reframed around accountability and validated with fresh polling.

### 6. Lobbying / Revolving-Door Restrictions

From [UMD PPC, June 2022](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/large-bipartisan-majority-favors-increasing-lobbying-restrictions-on-former-members-of-congress-and-other-government-officials/):

- Extend ex-members' lobbying wait, 2→5 years: **65% (65% R / 67% D)**
- Extend senior congressional staff wait: **74% (~75% R / ~75% D)**
- Extend executive-branch officials' wait to 5 years: **71% (72% R / 72% D)**
- Lifetime ban on ex-senior-officials lobbying for foreign governments: **71% (71% R / 74% D)**
- ⚠️ Lifetime ban on ALL congressional lobbying: only **32%** — the public wants cooling-off periods, not permanent bans.

Corroboration and context:

- Earlier wave: 5-year wait **79%**; lifetime foreign-government ban **75% (81% R / 70% D)** — [UMD PPC/Center for Public Integrity, Dec 2017](https://publicintegrity.org/politics/liberals-and-conservatives-agree-ex-congressmen-should-put-brakes-on-lobbying-careers/). Republicans run *ahead* of Democrats on foreign-lobbying bans in both waves.
- Lobbyists have too much influence on Congress: **73% (78% R / 72% D)** — [Pew, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/23/7-facts-about-americans-views-of-money-in-politics/).
- Corruption is a "big problem": **92% (90% R / 93% D / 93% I)**; new federal ethics-enforcement agency: 81% — [Brennan Center, April–May 2026](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/poll-voters-want-solutions-government-corruption) (advocacy-adjacent).
- American Anti-Corruption Act package: **87% (83% R / 91% D)** — [RepresentUs, c. 2018](https://represent.us/anticorruption-act/) (advocacy; treat as ceiling).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Most genuinely bipartisan category researched: gaps almost all under 10 pts; R lead D on foreign-lobbying bans.
- Nearly all specific revolving-door numbers trace to one shop (UMD PPC 2017 + 2022); other pollsters measure the problem, not the remedy — thin cross-pollster corroboration.

### Rejected 2026-08-06: Balanced Budget

- BBA: **80% (83% R / 79% D / 76% I)** — Harvard CAPS/Harris, July 2023, via [NSJ/Gingrich summary](https://nsjonline.com/article/2025/07/gingrich-the-american-people-want-a-balanced-budget/) (party split not verifiable on pollster's own site).
- BBA: **70% (74% R / 67% D / 70% I)** — [America's New Majority Project, 2025](https://americasnewmajorityproject.com/analysis-americans-want-a-return-to-balanced-budgets/) (Gingrich-affiliated advocacy); companion release: voters want balance [without raising taxes or cutting benefits](https://americasnewmajorityproject.com/voters-want-congress-to-balance-the-budget-without-raising-taxes-or-cutting-benefits/).
- BBA: **69%** — [Morning Consult/Cato via Newsweek, June 2026](https://www.newsweek.com/how-americans-would-change-the-constitution-new-poll-12152648) (no party split); **72%** — [Economist/YouGov, June 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54990-trumps-job-approval-rebounds-americans-lack-confidence-in-elections-abortion-views-and-more-june-13-15-2026-economist-yougov-poll).
- ⚠️ THE TRADE-OFF TEST: BBA favored 72–20 in the abstract → **31% favor / 63% oppose** if it required major Medicare/Social Security cuts (R: 82% → 47/46; D: 62% → 79% oppose); 62% oppose if it meant major tax increases — [Fox News, June 2011](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/fox-news-poll-balanced-budget-amendment-favored-obamas-stimulus-panned.amp). Old, but the only rigorous trade-off experiment found; **no 2020s pollster has rerun it**.
- Debt as top-3 priority: **77% (87% R / 73% D / 70% I)** — [Peterson Foundation, Sept 2024](https://www.pgpf.org/press/2024-9-fci-press-release/); Feb 2025: 88% R / 62% D / 71% I — [Peterson](https://www.pgpf.org/press/2025-02-fci-press-release/) (fiscal-hawk foundation).
- Deficit reduction a "top priority": 57% (71% R / 44% D) Jan 2023; 54% (68% R / 40% D) Jan 2024 — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/05/24/6-facts-about-americans-views-of-government-spending-and-the-deficit/), [Pew Feb 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/02/29/americans-top-policy-priority-for-2024-strengthening-the-economy/).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Topline bipartisanship is real but shallow: support falls ~40 pts once mechanisms are specified, and the coalition splits asymmetrically (D defect on entitlement cuts, R on taxes).
- Underlying urgency is R-tilted (24–28-pt gaps on deficit priority) even though BBA toplines look symmetric.
- For survey work: test the amendment WITH trade-off language — the unconditioned numbers overstate durable support.

### 7. Eliminate Mid-Decade Redistricting

- Only **9%** of Americans say states should be allowed to draw districts intentionally favoring one party — [Economist/YouGov, Aug 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52756-few-americans-support-texas-republicans-redistricting-plan-gerrymandering-opinions-split-democratic-lawmakers-move-stop-it-poll); but the Texas mid-decade plan itself: R approve 66/11, D disapprove 85/6.
- Nationwide ban on gerrymandering and mid-decade redistricting: **two-thirds, incl. majority of R and I** — [Common Cause, Sept 2025](https://www.commoncause.org/press/september-poll-64-of-republican-and-independent-voters-want-ban-for-mid-decade-redistricting/) (advocacy).
- Nonpartisan commissions should draw lines: **82%** — [NBC News, Aug–Sept 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/poll-americans-oppose-political-parties-drawing-election-lines-rcna229257) (party split paywalled).
- Trust commissions over legislatures: **41–16 overall (R 38–19; D 45–15)** — [Center Square Voters' Voice, Aug 2025](https://www.thecentersquare.com/national/article_020b50b6-b89b-4203-85e2-e7510ed95d96.html).
- Gerrymandering a "major problem": **65%** (D notably higher; just over half of R) — [AP-NORC, June 2023](https://apnorc.org/projects/partisan-views-of-the-electoral-system/).
- Redistricting fights "bad for democracy": **55% (46% R / 71% D)** — [Reuters/Ipsos, Aug 2025](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2025-08-21/americans-worry-democracy-in-danger-amid-gerrymandering-fights-reuters-ipsos-poll-finds).
- State-level perceived fairness varies: 66% call Texas's lines unfair vs. 35% for New York's; "avoiding partisan favoritism" is the public's top districting priority — [NORC, Feb 2026](https://www.norc.org/research/library/americans-opinions-gerrymandering-differ-party-affiliation.html).
- Hypocrisy check: 70% of Democrats say gerrymandering is "never acceptable," yet 63% backed California's Prop 50 counter-gerrymander and only 18% would keep California's commission if it cost their party seats — [Politico/UC Berkeley Citrin Center, Aug 2025](https://www.aol.com/majority-dems-gerrymandering-never-acceptable-141205887.html); Prop 50 voters still preferred commissions in principle — [Fox News Voter Poll, Oct–Nov 2025](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/fox-news-voter-poll-california-voters-ok-congressional-redistricting-plan).
- R subgroup for independent commissions: **57%** — [R Street via Campaign Legal Center, 2021](https://campaignlegal.org/update/poll-57-republicans-support-independent-redistricting-commissions); see also [CLC's bipartisan poll](https://campaignlegal.org/update/bipartisan-poll-shows-strong-support-redistricting-reform).

#### Nuances & caveats

- Dominant 2025 pattern: principled opposition + situational support. Each party's voters approve their own side's mid-decade map. Abstract "ban it" numbers overstate real-world resistance to co-partisan gerrymanders.
- Commission support is the sturdiest bipartisan finding, but soft under partisan pressure (the 18% Citrin number).
- A *mutual-disarmament* framing (both sides give up the weapon simultaneously — which is what a constitutional ban is) is the natural pitch and is exactly what polls haven't tested. Worth commissioning.

### 8. Presidential Pardon Transparency and Public Benefit

- Amend the Constitution to limit pardon power: **56% / 25% opposed (34% R / 76% D)** — [Economist/YouGov, Dec 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53702-majorities-americans-say-donald-trump-gives-too-many-pardons-want-to-limit-pardon-power-december-5-8-2025-economist-yougov-poll); **69%** in [Brennan Center, April–May 2026](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/poll-voters-want-solutions-government-corruption) (advocacy-adjacent, no split).
- Presidents should NOT pardon **family members**: **77% (64% R / 88% D / 77% I)**; staff/associates: **69% (50% R / 84% D)**; themselves: **78% (59% R / 96% D / 76% I)** — [YouGov for Protect Democracy, May–June 2024](https://protectdemocracy.org/work/survey-jan-6-pardon-opposition/) (advocacy-commissioned; battleground-district sample).
- Self-pardon norm predates current politics: ~75% of Republicans said a president shouldn't self-pardon if charged — [PBS/NPR/Marist, 2018](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/most-americans-oppose-presidential-self-pardons-poll-finds).
- Both parties' recent pardons are unpopular: Hunter Biden pardon 32% approve (12% R / 65% D) — [Monmouth, Dec 2024](https://www.monmouth.edu/polling-institute/reports/monmouthpoll_us_121124/), ~22% in AP-NORC ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5034478-hunter-biden-pardon-approval-poll/)); Trump associate pardons 29% approve — same Monmouth; Jan 6 blanket pardons ~60% oppose — [AP-NORC Jan 2025 via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/politics/who-supports-trumps-agenda-heres-what-an-ap-norc-poll-shows); violent-crime Jan 6 pardons 83% disapprove — [WaPo/Ipsos via The Hill, Feb 2025](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5157765-donald-trump-jan-6-pardons-wapo-survey/); deadly-weapon cases 75% oppose (55% R) — [Protect Democracy/YouGov, Jan 2025](https://protectdemocracy.org/work/new-poll-republicans-oppose-jan-6-pardons/).
- Specific 2025 pardons: Cuellar 66% disapprove, Hernández 66% disapprove; "Trump gives out too many pardons": 58% (23% R / 88% D) — [Economist/YouGov, Dec 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53702-majorities-americans-say-donald-trump-gives-too-many-pardons-want-to-limit-pardon-power-december-5-8-2025-economist-yougov-poll).

#### Nuances & caveats

- ⚠️ **No reputable poll directly tests "require a public written justification for each pardon"** — our category's core mechanism is a genuine polling gap worth commissioning. Earlier attribution of 79% to that proposal was the Brennan Center's broader corruption battery; treat the 79% as adjacent, not direct.
- Partisan mirror-imaging is extreme, and support for limits collapses within the incumbent president's party (34% R now; would likely flip under a D president). The durable bipartisan core: bans on self-pardons, family pardons, and pardons in violence-against-police cases (R majorities of 55–64% even in the current alignment).
- Fastest-moving opinion of any category (2024→2026), driven by both parties' controversial pardons — good timing for a consensus amendment framed as binding *all future presidents*.

### Age limits for judiciary and Congress

- Max age for elected officials: **79% (82% R / 76% D)** — [Pew, Oct 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/04/most-americans-favor-maximum-age-limits-for-federal-elected-officials-supreme-court-justices/); corroborated at **77% (79% R / 76% D)** — [CBS News/YouGov, Sept 2023](https://www.fourstateshomepage.com/hill-politics/most-americans-want-maximum-age-limit-for-elected-officials-poll/amp/) ([topline PDF](https://docs.cdn.yougov.com/ntoda8cxz7/cbsnews_20230910_1.pdf)); plurality would set the cap at 70.
- Max age for SCOTUS justices: **74% (68% R / 82% D)** — Pew Oct 2023; **67%** — AP-NORC July 2023 (via [YouGov's series](https://today.yougov.com/politics/articles/40527-elected-officials-maximum-age-limit-poll)).
- Presidential age cap: majority overall, **64% R / 88% D** in 2025 — a flip from 2022–23 when Republicans were more supportive — [YouGov, May 2025](https://today.yougov.com/politics/articles/52670-most-americans-dont-want-to-lower-the-voting-age-but-they-do-want-younger-presidents).
- Caveats: support drops ~10 pts when a specific cutoff must be chosen; partisan *intensity* migrates with the age of each party's standard-bearer, but the supermajority holds. **Strongest candidate category — consider promoting to target** (could fold into categories 2–3).

### Executive branch compliance with law and Congress

- Administration must obey a SCOTUS ruling: **~88% (82% R / 95% D)**; any federal court: **78% (65% R / 91% D)** — [Pew, April 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/04/23/views-of-how-the-trump-administration-governs/).
- President required to obey SCOTUS: **84% (78% R / 92% D / 76% I)** — [Marquette, Nov 2025](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2025/11/20/mlspsc29-court/), stable vs. [Sept 2025](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2025/10/02/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-continuing-large-majority-who-say-a-president-must-obey-the-supreme-court).
- Trump doing "too much" by executive order: 51% (21% R / 80% D) — Pew April 2025; "gone too far" with power: 57% — [AP-NORC, April 2025](https://apnorc.org/projects/many-feel-trump-has-gone-too-far-in-using-presidential-power-to-achieve-goals/).
- OMB funding freeze: 34% support / 62% oppose — [Reuters/Ipsos, Feb 2025](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2025-02-04/majority-of-americans-oppose-domestic-spending-freeze-sought-by-trump-reuters-ipsos-poll-finds) (no party split).
- Caveats: court-compliance is a genuinely strong cross-partisan principle (65–82% R), rising with court rank — but ~1 in 5 Republicans now say even SCOTUS can be ignored. **Impoundment (refusing to spend appropriated funds) is unpolled** — [GAO has called pocket rescissions illegal](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/trump_impoundment_eos_fact_sheet.pdf) but no pollster has asked. Strong potential category if framed as "every president must obey court rulings and spend what Congress appropriates."

### FBI/DOJ independence

- President should NOT direct DOJ to charge political opponents: **60% disagree with the power (36% R / 86% D / 53% I)** — [States United/YouGov, May 2026](https://statesunited.org/resources/justice-department-survey/) (advocacy-selected question, YouGov fieldwork); only ~1 in 5 endorse political use of DOJ — [States United, June 2025](https://statesunited.org/resources/politicized-prosecutions-rule-of-law/).

- Caveats: bipartisan opposition to the *abuse* in the abstract; no polling on a *structural* remedy (statutory/constitutional DOJ independence). Regime-coded in practice.

### FCC / independent-agency independence

- Pulling broadcast licenses from networks critical of the president: **12% support / 74% oppose (R 22/61)** — [YouGov 30-policy battery, Sept 2025, via Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/brutal-poll-shows-punishing-trump-critics-with-fcc-opposed-by-whopping-majority-even-of-trump-voters/); ranked last of all 30 policies tested. ### Filibuster reform

- Keep/reform/eliminate: 38/38/16 — [Monmouth, April 2021](https://www.monmouth.edu/polling-institute/reports/monmouthpoll_us_042921/); 43/35/17 — [Monmouth, Jan 2022](https://www.monmouth.edu/polling-institute/reports/monmouthpoll_us_012722/); keep: 68% R / 32% D — [Economist/YouGov, May 2021](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/36290-supporting-senate-filibuster-poll) ("Party is all that matters").

### Civil servant independence

- Nonpartisan civil service important for democracy: **67% in 2025, down from 87% in 2024 (R 87→66; I 79→34)** — [Partnership for Public Service, spring 2025, via GovExec](https://www.govexec.com/workforce/2025/08/fewer-republicans-view-nonpartisan-civil-service-essential-survey-shows/407403/).

- Caveats: R support for merit protections is collapsing in real time; "Schedule F" polls terribly as a term (no name recognition). Currently regime-coded — hard full-spectrum case. Also recall UMD PPC: extending a stock-trading ban to all federal employees drops support to 40% — the public doesn't extend elite-accountability instincts to the civil service.

### Unpolled candidates

- **Fair single-member districting / proportional representation / fusion voting**: no reputable 2020–2026 national polls with party splits. Closest proxies: ~two-thirds "want more parties" — [New America](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/governing-the-house-with-multiple-parties/proportional-representation-and-multipartyism-in-the-united-states/); [Protect Democracy fusion-voting explainers](https://protectdemocracy.org/work/fusion-voting-explained/). Transparency is a valence issue; would likely poll 70–90% but no one has tested a concrete mandate.

### Extends: 2. Term Limits and Ethics Rules for Supreme Court & Federal Judiciary

- Term limits for justices: **75% (67% R / 76% I / 85% D)**, 22% disagree — [PRRI 2025 American Values Survey, Aug 15–Sept 8, 2025 (n=5,543)](https://prri.org/research/trumps-unprecedented-actions-deepen-asymmetric-divides/). Fills in the party split for the PRRI figure the memo cites without one; a 67% R floor from a large-sample independent pollster.
- Marquette's own trend line for "fixed terms": **71% (2019) → high of 83% (Oct 2024: 70% R / 86% I / 94% D) → 79% (May 2026: 73% R / 71% I / 87% D)** — [Marquette Oct 2024](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2024/10/17/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-approval-of-u-s-supreme-court-edges-upward-but-55-disapprove-of-the-courts-work/), [Marquette May 2026 release](https://law.marquette.edu/assets/community/poll/MLSPSC32/MLSPSC32PressRelease_CourtIssues.pdf). Note the R number has been essentially flat (70–73%) while D support fell 7 pts — the topline decline is on the D side.
- Court expansion, May 2026 contrast case: **50/50 overall; 61% of R opposed, 62% of D in favor**; support has held between 48% and 54% across nine Marquette polls since 2021 — same release. Keeps the rejection intact.

- Binding ethics/disclosure law for justices: **84% / 9% oppose** — Brennan/Verasight April–May 2026 [toplines](https://www.brennancenter.org/media/15709/download/corruption-survey-toplines-2026-06-01-final.pdf) (no party split published; consistent with Marquette's 90%).

### Extends: 3. Term Limits and Ethics Rules for Congress

- Party splits for the Economist/YouGov May 22–26, 2026 wording experiment the memo already cites: "not allowed to trade individual stocks" **76% (80% D / 72% I / 77% R)**; "banned" **71%**, with Democrats showing the largest wording gap (80% → 69%) and R/I little or none — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54851-most-americans-think-congressional-corruption-is-widespread-may-22-26-2026-economist-yougov-poll). R ≥ D on the softer wording; corruption perceptions comparable across parties (~one-third of each say most members are corrupt; 49% of independents).
- The July 22, 2026 House vote the memo cites as "momentum" was **232–198, mostly party-line (13 Democrats in favor)**: the bill bars members/spouses/dependents from *new* purchases, allows existing holdings with 7–14 days' notice, and had a federal voter-ID requirement attached that Democrats called a poison pill — [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/07/22/congressional-stock-trading-bill-passes-the-house/). A cross-party *vote* test this is not; the polling consensus (86–87% after deliberation, 77% R quick-poll) has not yet produced a bipartisan floor vote.

### Extends: 4. Ethics Rules for President

- The memo says emoluments enforcement is "essentially unpolled." Brennan/Verasight April 28–May 6, 2026 tested it directly: a law empowering Congress and the states to sue the president and federal officials for violating the constitutional bars on gifts from foreign countries and U.S. states — **71% support / 18% oppose** (the lowest support and highest opposition of the nine reforms tested) — [toplines](https://www.brennancenter.org/media/15709/download/corruption-survey-toplines-2026-06-01-final.pdf). No party split published; treat as a first data point, not a floor. Exact wording of the 83% conflicts item: "a law that bars presidents from having conflicts of interest and holds them to broadly the same ethics standards as other federal employees" (83/10).

### Extends: 7. Eliminate Mid-Decade Redistricting

- Newest independent read on the principle: states should **not** be allowed to draw districts intentionally favoring one party — **71% (74% D / 70% I / 69% R)**, only 7% say it should be allowed — [Economist/YouGov, April 24–27, 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54644-most-americans-say-partisan-gerrymandering-should-not-be-allowed-april-24-27-2026-economist-yougov-poll). On the principle framing the R floor is ~69%, well above the ~55% summary floor — but the same poll shows each party blames the other roughly 3-to-1 for unfair maps, and the ballot record below shows the situational-defection pattern is intact. **Bears on the verdict:** the honest floor depends on whether the question is "gerrymandering" (≈69% R) or "your side's counter-map" (majority approval).
- Real-world tests, 2024–2026, all pointing the same way as the Prop 50 finding: **California Prop 50** (Nov 4, 2025) passed **64.4–35.6** (Democratic counter-map, temporary through 2030) — [certified result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_California_Proposition_50); **Virginia's April 21, 2026 referendum** authorizing a mid-decade Democratic map passed only **51.7–48.3** and was **struck down 4–3 by the Supreme Court of Virginia on May 8, 2026** for defects in the amendment procedure — [Virginia Mercury](https://virginiamercury.com/2026/05/08/supreme-court-of-virginia-strikes-down-redistricting-amendment-keeps-current-maps-in-place/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/08/virginia-redistricting-decision-supreme-court.html); and **Ohio Issue 1** (Nov 5, 2024), the Citizens Not Politicians independent-commission amendment, **failed 53.7–46.3** after a Ballot Board summary the sponsors sued over as "biased, inaccurate, [and] deceptive" (Ohio Supreme Court ordered only minor changes) — [Wikipedia summary of certified result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ohio_Issue_1). Lesson for the gate: 82% "commission" polling did not survive hostile ballot language in a red state, and voters in a blue state approved a co-partisan gerrymander 2-to-1 while telling pollsters they oppose gerrymandering — a *mutual-disarmament* amendment is the only framing that has not yet been tested at the ballot box.

The article does not publish the percentages; the specific "must the president spend what Congress appropriates?" question is still uncommissioned.
