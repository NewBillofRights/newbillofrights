# An enforceable emoluments clause — Clause Memo (Ethics)

*Held back in [research/ETHICS_RESEARCH.md](../../ETHICS_RESEARCH.md); tracked as a candidate clause of the Ethics amendment from 2026-08-18. Every figure verified against the linked source.*

*Changelog: 2026-08-18 — figures re-verified; 1 correction applied in place (git history has the diff).*

**Status in one line:** Unpolled on the remedy (71% overall, no party split) — the drafting is done and the structural case is the strongest in the ethics package, but every fielded framing of the underlying conduct splits by 45+ points and the newest reading is worse, not better — **candidate clause (gate: neutral both-parties polling on emoluments enforcement)**.

## 1. The clause

The text held back in the parent memo:

> *"The prohibitions of this article and of the emoluments clauses of this Constitution shall be enforceable by law…"*

**What it would add.** The ethics article as drafted has five sections and no general enforcement clause — no designated enforcer, no standing rule, no remedy. The parent memo's implementation pass lists this as open litigation question 4: *"Who enforces Sections 1, 2, and 4, and with what standing… no remedy for a Section 1 violation (fine? forfeiture? disqualification?)."* This clause is item (v) in the package skeleton — a one-sentence enforcement and standing authorization — and reaches backward to the Constitution's existing emoluments clauses (Art. I, § 9, cl. 8; Art. II, § 1, cl. 7), real prohibitions with no working remedy ([CRS IF11086](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF11086/IF11086.15.pdf)).

**Why it was held back.** Not the merits and not the drafting. All three emoluments suits of 2017–2021 died on *procedure*: the Supreme Court dismissed *CREW v. Trump* as moot on January 25, 2021 and vacated the lower-court rulings, leaving no surviving precedent ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/regulation/court-battles/535668-supreme-court-dismisses-emoluments-lawsuits-against-trump-as-moot/); [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2021/01/justices-vacate-rulings-on-trump-and-emoluments/)); *Blumenthal v. Trump* fell on standing, the D.C. Circuit holding on February 7, 2020 that individual members of Congress could not sue ([949 F.3d 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumenthal_v._Trump)); and the D.C./Maryland suit ran the full procedural gauntlet without ever reaching the merits — a Fourth Circuit panel ordered it dismissed for want of standing on July 10, 2019 ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2019/07/10/740258653/appeals-court-dismisses-emoluments-lawsuit-against-trump)), the en banc court vacated that panel opinion and revived the suit 9–6 on May 14, 2020 (*In re Trump*, [959 F.3d 126](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/18-2488/18-2488-2020-05-14.html); [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/en-banc-4th-circuit-revives-emoluments-suit-against-trump)), and the Supreme Court then vacated that judgment under *Munsingwear* and ordered dismissal as moot once the term ended — mootness, not standing, was its terminal defect. Standing was therefore contested and never settled: one suit lost it, the other survived it and lost the case anyway. The parent memo's lesson from that record — *constitutional ethics text without a designated enforcer is a parchment barrier* — is now carried platform-wide. The hold was purely the evidence gate.

## 2. The evidence

- **The remedy, asked directly — the only reading that exists.** "A law that empowers Congress and the states to sue the president and federal officials for violating constitutional provisions that bar them from accepting gifts from foreign countries and U.S. states": **71% support / 18% oppose**. Brennan Center / Verasight, April 28–May 6, 2026, n=2,000 RV, ±2.2 — [toplines](https://www.brennancenter.org/media/15709/download/corruption-survey-toplines-2026-06-01-final.pdf). **No party crosstabs published**: party ID is collected (Q.22) but no breakdowns appear for the proposals battery (Q.15). Advocacy-commissioned, and by the sponsor's own battery the *lowest* support and *highest* opposition of nine reforms tested. A first data point, not a floor.

- **The conduct, with party splits — the newest reading, and it moved the wrong way.** "Do you think it is acceptable or unacceptable for a U.S. president to accept valuable gifts from foreign governments while in office?": **61% unacceptable / 22% acceptable — 82% D / 69% I / 33% R unacceptable**, Republicans **46% acceptable**, a plurality. Economist/YouGov, August 14–17, 2026, n=1,611 ([crosstabs Q35, p. 53](https://d3nkl3psvxxpe9.cloudfront.net/documents/econTabReport_ZMy1Zxu.pdf)). Independent pollster; generic, forward-neutral wording naming no officeholder and no transaction. **New to the corpus as of this memo**, and the cleanest measurement of the emoluments principle anywhere in it.

- **The prior anchor, on a specific transaction.** A foreign-state aircraft gift "raises ethical concerns about corruption": **62% — 40% R / 85% D / 62% I**. Harvard CAPS/Harris, May 14–15, 2025, n=1,903 RV — [press release](https://harvardharrispoll.com/press-release-may-2025/). Re-verified for this memo.

- **The neighbors that clear the bar** — the contrast defining the problem. Extending the trading and conflict-of-interest ban up to the President, VP, and Justices: **87% (87% R / 90% D)** — [UMD PPC, May 2023](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/stock-trading-by-members-of-congress/). Judicial ethics enforcement: **90% (88% R / 93% D)** — [Marquette, October 2024](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2024/10/17/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-approval-of-u-s-supreme-court-edges-upward-but-55-disapprove-of-the-courts-work/). Barring officials and families from crypto profits in office: **71% (67% R)**, holding at 66% (55% R) after counterargument — [PPI, 2025](https://www.progressivepolicy.org/as-trump-pockets-1-2-billion-from-crypto-new-poll-finds-voters-want-the-industry-and-self-dealing-officials-reined-in/) *(center-left advocacy)*. The collapse is specific to foreign payments, not to presidential ethics generally.

**Nuances and caveats.** The 71% and the 33% are not in conflict — one asks about a procedure, the other about conduct, and a respondent can find the conduct acceptable while still favoring a rule that lets courts decide. Whether the 71% survives a party crossing is unknown because it was never published. The August 2026 number is the most important fact here and the most discouraging: between May 2025 and August 2026 the Republican reading did not recover toward the neutral framing, it sat at or below the transaction-specific one. Generic wording did not de-code the issue. The pattern is **abuse-versus-remedy with a twist** — the standard shape is shared diagnosis, split remedy; here a Republican plurality no longer condemns the abuse, making this harder than DOJ independence (60% oppose the abuse, 36% R on the fix). Regime coding is unmeasurable: every framing since 2017 was fielded while the issue was live and person-coded, and the corpus has no cold-issue baseline (§11, "same principle, different incumbent"). A live legislative vehicle exists — [H.R. 9222 (119th)](https://www.govinfo.gov/bulkdata/BILLSTATUS/119/hr/BILLSTATUS-119hr9222.xml) bundles emoluments strengthening with much of the platform, statutorily — but it is a one-member messaging bill with no cross-party vote.

## 3. The gate result

**Floor: none — Unpolled.** No poll has asked about emoluments enforcement with a published party breakdown. The one remedy reading (71%) has no crosstab; the one recent principle reading with a crosstab (33% R) is about conduct. Treating that as the proxy floor would yield **Weak** — disqualifying every time it has appeared in this corpus.

**Failure modes (§4.3).** *Abuse-versus-remedy* — **failing, in its most severe observed form**; there is no shared diagnosis to build a remedy on. *High-topline / low-floor* — **at maximum risk**: a 71% topline with an unpublished floor, adjacent to a matched-subject item at 33% R. *Framing dependency* — **the open question and the whole memo**: the parent's path back assumes an honest framing exists, and August 2026 is the first direct evidence against that assumption, since neutral generic wording is already in the field and returns 33% R. What remains untested is whether adding the enforceability mechanism moves it — the 33-to-71 gap is the space a commissioned poll must explore. *Incumbent/regime coding* — at risk, untestable now. *Depth* — untested; no survey has put the counter (a hostile Congress or state AG suing any future president over ordinary dealings) to respondents. *Staleness* — **cleared**; the evidence is days old.

**Non-polling tests (§5).** As a clause its why-an-amendment answer is usually the parent's (answer 2 — § 208 exempts exactly these offices), but here it also **passes independently on answer 4, the text is silent**: the emoluments clauses already *are* constitutional text and still failed, because nothing says who may enforce them or who has standing. Three suits, zero merits rulings, all precedent vacated; no statute can confer standing Article III forecloses, which is why the durable version is constitutional. *Symmetry* — **passes by construction, fails in reception**: the text names no one and binds every future officeholder, and the clause runs to "the prohibitions of this article," so the enforcement authorization would apply to Congress and the judiciary as much as the presidency — yet the August 2026 wording is already symmetric and still returns a 49-point gap. Symmetric drafting is necessary and demonstrably not sufficient. *Existential-problem* — **passes**: the defect is prohibitions without enforcers, which the corpus has diagnosed twice, in this memo's own Sections 1–4 and in [SALON_TOPICS.md](../../SALON_TOPICS.md) as the case against a declaratory executive-compliance amendment.

## 4. Poll question

**Core item (verbatim):**

> "The Constitution bars presidents and other federal officials from accepting payments or gifts from foreign governments, but courts have never ruled on whether anyone can sue to enforce that ban. Would you favor or oppose a constitutional amendment stating that this ban is enforceable in court — so that any future president of either party who accepts payments from a foreign government could be required to answer for it in a lawsuit?"

**Follow-up 1 — costs named (required; the depth test is untested):**

> "Supporters say a rule no one can enforce is not a rule. Opponents say it would let a hostile Congress or a state attorney general drag any president into court over ordinary business dealings, and that presidents of both parties would be sued for political reasons. Knowing both arguments, do you favor or oppose the amendment?"

**Follow-up 2 — separating conduct from remedy (diagnostic; explains the 71/33 gap), asked after the core item so it does not prime it:**

> "Regardless of whether anyone should be able to sue, do you think it is acceptable or unacceptable for a president of either party to accept valuable gifts from a foreign government while in office?"

**Threshold: ≥60% in both parties on the core item, and ≥55% in both parties surviving Follow-up 1.** At that result the clause folds into the ethics article as the enforcement section at low drafting cost — the text is written and the package skeleton reserves the slot. A Moderate 45–59% R floor keeps it a candidate clause and argues for drafting the enforcement authorization *without* the emoluments cross-reference (§5). Below 45% in either party, or a drop of more than 15 points under Follow-up 1, moves it to rejected-with-a-path-back.

**Design requirements:** field it in an instrument also carrying the ethics package's own 87% R / 90% D presidential-extension item, so the gap between "presidential ethics" and "presidential emoluments" is measured within the same respondents; name no officeholder and no transaction; publish the crosstabs. **Cheapest first step: request the party crosstabs for Brennan/Verasight Q.15.** Party ID was collected but not published, so the breakdown of the only remedy reading in existence may already be in hand at zero fieldwork cost — that request could resolve this clause faster than a new survey.

## 5. Recommendation

**Candidate clause.** It has the best why-an-amendment answer of any held-back item in the corpus — the emoluments clauses are the domestic proof that prohibitions without enforcers do not work, and the ethics article currently repeats that mistake in its own text — and the worst reception evidence, which just got worse rather than better. It stays a candidate rather than a rejection because the remedy has genuinely never been asked with a party split, and the one time it was asked at all it drew 71%.

One available split the founder should note: **the enforcement authorization and the emoluments cross-reference can be severed.** "The prohibitions of this article shall be enforceable by law" answers open litigation question 4, carries none of the foreign-payments coding, and rides on the package's own 87% R support; "and of the emoluments clauses of this Constitution" is the half carrying the 33% floor. On a Moderate result, drafting the first half now and holding the second is the disposition the evidence supports.
