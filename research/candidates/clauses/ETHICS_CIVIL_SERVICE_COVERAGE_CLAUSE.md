# Extending the trading ban to all federal employees — Clause Memo (Ethics)

*Held back in [research/ETHICS_RESEARCH.md](../../ETHICS_RESEARCH.md); tracked as a rejected clause (support drops 86%→40% when extended to all federal employees, UMD 2023 — verify; substantiate; path back or none) clause of the Ethics amendment from 2026-08-18. Every figure verified against the linked source.*

*Changelog: 2026-08-18 — figures re-verified; 1 correction applied in place (git history has the diff).*

**Status in one line:** floor **37%** (Democrats) — **Weak, and the only item in the corpus rejected by consensus rather than by party split** — what holds it is that both parties reached the same answer at nearly the same rate, on a counterargument Democrats found *more* convincing than Republicans did — **rejected clause (test: none identified; the finding is affirmative, not a gap in the evidence)**.

## 1. The clause

There is no drafted text, and that is part of the record. This clause exists in the corpus only as the *rejected breadth* of Principle 1 — the boundary the anti-self-dealing rule is drawn against, not a variant written and set aside. Principle 1 reads:

> *"No person holding the office of President or Vice President, no Senator or Representative, and no Justice or judge of the United States shall, during their continuance in office, purchase, sell, or hold any individual security, digital asset, or contract of speculation whose value their official conduct may affect, other than through a diversified fund or qualified blind trust as Congress shall by law provide."*

The clause would widen that covered class to the roughly two million federal employees, everything else — safe harbor, delegation to Congress — carrying over unchanged.

**Why the parent memo held it back.** ETHICS_RESEARCH.md logs it as the **breadth trap**: *"the covered class must stop at elected/appointed constitutional officers — extending to civil servants collapses support from 86% to 40%. Resist every 'why not all federal employees?' amendment-by-committee impulse; the RI model's universal coverage is the wrong federal template."* The strategic read makes it a drafting rule: *"Lead with Congress, extend upward, never downward."* Rhode Island complicates it — a 1986 constitutional Ethics Commission covering *all* state and municipal officials and employees, re-ratified by a wide margin in 2016 — and the memo answers: employee-level enforcement there is rare and uncontroversial, but the federal amendment stays narrow.

## 2. The evidence

**One instrument, and it is a good one.** UMD Program for Public Consultation, fielded **May 19–30, 2023**, n=**2,625 registered voters**, ±**1.9%**, sample from Nielsen Scarborough — a *deliberative* format in which respondents read the proposal, evaluated an argument for and one against, rated acceptability, and only then gave a favor/oppose verdict ([PPC](https://publicconsultation.org/united-states/stock-trading-by-members-of-congress/); [questionnaire with full crosstabs](https://publicconsultation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/StockTradesQuaire_0623.pdf)).

**Three items, same instrument, same scale:**

| Item | Favor | R | D | I | Floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q6 — ban Members of Congress and resident family from trading individual stocks | **86.2%** | 86.6% | 88.3% | 80.5% | **86.6%** |
| Q8 — extend to the President, Vice President, and Supreme Court Justices | **87.3%** | 86.9% | 90.0% | 81.9% | **86.9%** |
| **Q12 — extend to all federal employees** | **39.9%** | **41.6%** | **37.1%** | **42.2%** | **37.1%** |

Opposition on Q12 was **59.2%** (57.0R/62.2D/57.1I). This is a **standalone favor/oppose item**, not a forced choice and not a cumulative total — it does not carry the defect SELECTION_METHOD §11 records, which required the lobbying figures to be corrected on 2026-08-17.

**Exact wording of the rejected item** (Q11/Q12 stem, verbatim; sourced to the PORTFOLIO Act, H.R. 389):

> *"One more proposal is to prohibit all federal employees from trading stocks in individual companies. Currently, for senior employees in federal agencies, there are ethics rules against owning any stocks which could create a conflict of interest, and ethics boards which determine whether that is the case... [they] would have to either: Sell them — OR — Put them in a 'blind trust.'"*

**The finding that changes the clause's status: the party split is five points wide.** Highest to lowest party figure on Q12 is **5.1 points** (42.2 I / 41.6 R / 37.1 D). Compare the corpus's actual partisan-split rejections — Electoral College 46R/80D (34 points), self-pardon ban 43R/82D (39), Qatar emoluments 40R/85D (45). **This clause was not rejected by one party but by both, at nearly identical rates, Democrats lowest.** That disposition is unique in the registry, and it is why no path back exists.

**The counterargument, and who found it convincing.** The argument *against* (Q10) read:

> *"The federal government employs nearly 2 million people... Most are just ordinary workers... They do not have access to special information or make important decisions. And for senior officials who do have access to that kind of information, there are already stringent rules in place... It is unnecessary government overreach to put these restrictions on all those workers."*

Convincing to **71.0% — 68.2R/75.0D/68.6I**. The argument *in favor* (Q9, "there should not be exceptions... there should be a strict ban") was convincing to **64.5% — 65.0R/63.4D/66.3I**. Two things follow. On the same battery where the congressional ban's pro-argument drew **92.3%** (92.3R/92.8D), this version drew 64.5% — the argument is weaker, not just the proposal. And the counterargument beat the case in favor **in every party**, widest among **Democrats** (75.0 vs. 63.4). Neither coalition declined this on grounds available to only one side.

**Nuances & caveats**

- **Corpus correction.** REGISTRY_INVENTORY_2026-08-18.md §A and §C, POLLING_RESEARCH.md line 99, and the amendment page all record this as "40%, no party breakdown published." **The breakdown is published** — in the questionnaire PDF linked from PPC's own page, and already reproduced correctly in [CIVIL_SERVICE_CANDIDATE.md](../CIVIL_SERVICE_CANDIDATE.md) §2. The registry `floor` object should carry `rep: 41.6, dem: 37.1, ind: 42.2, overall: 39.9` with the URL. Precise figures are 39.9 / 41.6 / 37.1 / 42.2, not the rounded 40 / 42 / 37 / 42; rounding is fine on the page, but the floor is 37%.
- **Deliberation moved this proposal down, not up.** The congressional ban is format-sensitive the other way (~63% quick, 86–87% deliberative). The same treatment that lifted Q6 to 86% left Q12 at 40% — respondents given the case for it still declined. The depth test returned a verdict rather than going untested.
- **One pollster, one wave, 2023 fieldwork.** Normally an argument for a re-poll; §4 explains why not here.
- **Rhode Island cuts against the finding without overcoming it.** RI voters were never asked the federal question, employee-level enforcement there is rare, and a state ballot result on an enforcement body is not a national reading on a trading ban.
- **Scope is the mechanism, not the wording.** The clause reaches two million households' savings, including people with no access to nonpublic information, and forces sale or blind trust for all of them. The polling measures that accurately.

## 3. The gate result

- **Bipartisan floor: 37.1% (Democrats)** — the lower of the two parties per §4.1 → **Weak**. It is Weak in *both* parties: Republicans at 41.6% are also below the 45% line. No framing in the record clears any bar.
- **Failure modes (§4.3):**
  - *Depth (trade-off collapse)* — **applies; the operative test.** The cost was named in the instrument (overreach on two million ordinary workers; senior employees already covered) and support did not survive it. The balanced-budget shape, except the un-costed version was never popular either.
  - *Blast radius (§6.3)* — **applies.** The clause cannot be scoped without losing its point: the point *is* the breadth, and the breadth is what the public rejects.
  - *High-topline / low-floor* — **does not apply, and its absence is the finding.** No topline hides a split; topline and both party figures agree.
  - *Framing dependency* — **does not apply on this record.** One framing has been fielded, but the argument battery shows why a better one is unlikely: the pro-argument underperformed its congressional twin by 28 points in the same survey.
  - *Incumbent / regime coding* — **does not apply.** Nothing turns on who holds power; the two-million-worker objection is administration-independent.
  - *Staleness* — nominally applies (2023). A re-poll would need a theory of what changed; none is on offer.
- **Non-polling tests (§5):**
  - *Existential-problem (§5.1)* — **fails.** Conflicts among rank-and-file employees are a management problem, not a threat to the country's future. The instrument's own framing concedes senior agency employees are already covered by [18 U.S.C. § 208](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/208) and agency ethics boards — and § 208's conspicuous *exemptions* are precisely the President, Vice President, Members of Congress, and federal judges. The gap the ethics amendment exists to close is at the top, not the bottom.
  - *Symmetry (§5.2)* — **passes, and passing does not help it.** Universal coverage binds everyone by construction. A useful demonstration that symmetry is necessary, not sufficient.
  - *Why-an-amendment (§5.3)* — a clause normally inherits the parent's answer, here answer 2: § 208 exempts exactly those offices, so only an amendment reaches them. **The inheritance fails**, because that answer is specific to the exempted class. For everyone else § 208 already applies, Congress can amend it by statute, no court has struck it, and the PORTFOLIO Act (H.R. 389) is the live statutory vehicle. No why-an-amendment answer of its own — an independent disqualification before any polling is consulted.

## 4. Path back — none identified

**No poll question is written, and that is the recommendation rather than an omission.** Every other rejected item in the corpus carries a path back because its failure was a *gap* — a partisan split a better framing might close, a stale reading fresh fieldwork might revive, an unmeasured mechanism. This clause's failure is an *affirmative finding*: asked deliberatively, given the strongest argument in its favor, both parties said no at nearly the same rate, and the objection that moved them is structural to the proposal rather than an artifact of wording.

**What would have to be true for it to reopen** — stated so the record is falsifiable, not as a survey to commission:

1. **A different proposal, not a different framing** — a narrow clause reaching *senior* career officials with access to nonpublic market-moving information. Those people are already inside the § 208 regime, and it would have to answer §5.3 on its own; drafting it would not rehabilitate this clause.
2. **A demonstrated failure of the statutory floor** — evidence that § 208 and agency ethics boards are systematically failing at the senior-career level. That is an argument for enforcement, which Principle 4's independence clause already carries.
3. **A national reading clearing 60% in both parties on the operative breadth.** At 41.6R/37.1D after deliberation, this is not a near miss.

Nothing in the 2026 record supplies any of the three. **Searched for and not found (2026-08-18):** any poll after May 2023, from any house, asking whether a stock-trading ban should extend to all or to career federal employees; any party-broken reading of the PORTFOLIO Act's coverage; any state ballot measure applying a trading ban to public employees generally. The July 2026 House-passed congressional trading bill ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/07/22/congressional-stock-trading-bill-passes-the-house/)) covers Members, not the workforce — the legislative record moves the direction the polling points: upward and narrow.

## 5. Recommendation

**Rejected clause — confirm the rejection, and record it as the corpus's cleanest one.** The 37% Democratic floor is the honest number, both parties sit below 45%, the clause fails the existential-problem test on its own terms, and it has no why-an-amendment answer independent of the parent's — three disqualifications, any one sufficient. This answers registry §E.1 for this entry: rejection, not hold, carrying `pollQuestion: null` with an explicit "none identified" rather than an empty gate.

Two corpus changes follow. The party breakdown **is** published and should replace "no party breakdown published" in the registry entry, POLLING_RESEARCH.md line 99, and the amendment page — the site currently understates its own evidence. And this clause is worth keeping visible precisely *because* it is a consensus rejection: it is the best demonstration in the corpus that the gate measures something real rather than filtering for partisan comfort. The narrow covered class is not a compromise the polling forced on the amendment; it is the proposal the public actually holds.
