# Independent Enforcement of Court Judgments — Clause Memo (Executive Compliance with the Courts)
*Held back in [research/EXECUTIVE_COMPLIANCE_RESEARCH.md](../../EXECUTIVE_COMPLIANCE_RESEARCH.md); tracked as a candidate clause of the Executive Compliance with the Courts amendment from 2026-08-18. Every figure verified against the linked source.*

*Changelog: 2026-08-18 — figures re-verified; 1 correction applied in place (git history has the diff).*

**Status in one line:** Unpolled — the mechanism has never been measured in any independent survey, and the design work behind it is unfinished — what holds it is that the clause's own supporting analysis concedes the circularity it exists to close never fully closes — **candidate clause (gate: unpolled; separation-of-powers design unfinished)**.

## 1. The clause

The drafted text, retained in full in the parent memo's held-back block:

> *"The execution and enforcement of the judgments and orders of the courts of the United States shall be under the direction of the judicial branch. Officers charged with such enforcement shall be appointed and supervised as provided by law, but shall not, in the performance of that duty, be subject to the direction of any officer whose conduct is the subject of the judgment or order."*

**What it would add.** The amendment as drafted ([PROPOSED_AMENDMENTS.md](../../PROPOSED_AMENDMENTS.md) §7) has two declaratory sections — tiered bindingness and a safe harbor — and neither changes who carries out a judgment when an officer declines to. Today that answer is statutory: [28 U.S.C. §566](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A28+section%3A566+edition%3Aprelim%29) makes enforcing court orders the Marshals Service's "primary role" while placing it under the Attorney General; criminal contempt is prosecuted by an attorney for the government ([Rule 42(a)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcrmp/rule_42)) and is pardonable (*Ex parte Grossman*, 1925); civil contempt fines against an agency come out of the Treasury. Every path runs through the branch the judgment is against.

The clause moves execution to the judicial branch constitutionally while leaving structure to Congress "by law" (§6.2). Its second sentence is narrower than a wholesale transfer: it disqualifies from directing enforcement only the officer whose own conduct is the subject of the judgment. That conflict-targeted design is what makes the clause defensible, and also what makes it smaller than the problem it is named for.

**Why it was held back.** Two reasons, one of which is not polling. No survey has asked who should carry out a court's judgment — but the parent's trap note goes further: *"the who-enforces circularity never fully closes."* Officers with arrest power over executive officials, commanded by judges, create their own separation-of-powers problem. The memo names the honest design goal — remove the executive's *veto* over enforcement, not create a judicial police force — and does not finish the drafting that would secure the first without producing the second. The inventory is exact: *"design work as much as polling."* This is the one held-back clause where a clean poll would not by itself resolve the hold.

## 2. The evidence

**Best first: the anchor this clause sits under is Strong, and it is not this clause.** Marquette's [January 21–28, 2026 wave](https://today.marquette.edu/2026/02/new-marquette-law-school-national-survey-finds-63-say-u-s-supreme-court-should-limit-trumps-tariff-authority/) (n=1,003): **82% say a president must obey a Supreme Court decision — 76% R / 79% I / 90% D**, "quite stable in 10 Marquette polls since 2019, never dipping below 76%." The [November 5–12, 2025 wave](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2025/11/20/mlspsc29-court/) (n=1,052): **84% — 78% R / 76% I / 92% D**. That measures the duty, not the remedy.

**Direct evidence for this clause: none.** A search on 2026-08-18 for polling on moving court-order enforcement to the judiciary, on the Marshals Service's branch placement, or on who should execute a judgment against an executive officer found no survey — independent or advocacy — asking any of them. What it returns instead is the legislative record: the [MARSHALS Act](https://www.booker.senate.gov/news/press/booker-schumer-padilla-schiff-raskin-swalwell-and-johnson-introduce-bicameral-bill-to-move-us-marshals-service-to-judicial-branch) has **no Republican sponsor** in either chamber. The only existing vehicle for the idea is single-party — the opposite of the sponsorship pattern the corpus treats as full-spectrum evidence.

**Nuances and caveats.**

*The tier problem lands harder here.* Marquette's November 2025 wave asked whether court orders temporarily blocking a sitting administration's actions are a proper use of judicial authority: **65% overall, 39% R / 71% I / 89% D**. The parent tiers section 1 on this — unconditional at the apex, procedural below. An enforcement clause cannot tier the same way: execution is overwhelmingly a district-court function, so a judicially-directed corps would enforce exactly the lower-tier orders where the Republican floor sits near 40%. It inherits the weaker number.

*The circularity is real but partial, and enforcement fails at a different stage.* Rule 42(a)(2) already lets a court appoint a private prosecutor when the government declines (*Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton*). Meanwhile cabinet officers have been held in contempt under administrations of both parties — two cabinet secretaries in 1999 and a third in 2002 in [Cobell](https://intercontinentalcry.org/cobell-v-norton-overview-and-chronology/) ([reversed 2004](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dc-circuit/1344579.html)), all under a Democratic and then a Republican administration; and a cabinet secretary in [*Calvillo Manriquez*](https://www.forbes.com/sites/zackfriedman/2019/10/24/betsy-devos-student-loans-contempt/) (2019), sanction paid by the Department. In every case the failure was at the *consequence* stage, not the *execution* stage — no case turned on marshals refusing to serve process. That lowers the clause's marginal value and points at the consequences clause as the higher-value machinery.

*One live variable.* The *J.G.G.* contempt inquiry was frozen, terminated, then revived — [en banc D.C. Circuit, June 22, 2026](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/orders/docs/2026/06/25-5452CCEN.pdf), argument September 29, 2026 — and will change what this clause must do.

## 3. The gate result

**Floor: none. Verdict: Unpolled** (§4.1) — no credible national data on the mechanism, and the search that would have found it found the absence instead.

**Failure modes (§4.3):**
- *Abuse-vs-remedy gap* — **the operative one, and the clause is on the wrong side.** Both parties condemn the abuse (76–78% R / 90–92% D on the duty); the remedy has never been asked. Same shape as FBI/DOJ independence (60% oppose the abuse, 36% R on the fix) — candidates, not targets, for exactly this reason.
- *Incumbent / regime coding* — **high risk, unmeasured.** A question about judges commanding officers with power over executive officials will be answered against whoever currently holds the White House. Marquette's ten-wave stability protects the duty item, not this one.
- *Framing dependency* — **at risk, untested.** "Courts should be able to enforce their own orders" and "judges should command officers who can arrest cabinet officials" are the same clause and would not poll within twenty points.
- *High-topline/low-floor* and *depth* — unassessable and untested; the 39% R lower-court figure is the warning. *Staleness* — nothing to be stale.

**Non-polling tests (§5):**
- *Why-an-amendment (§5.3)* — **a clause normally inherits the parent's answer; this inheritance is weak.** The parent clears on answer 4 (the text is silent, and that silence is what defiant lawyers cite). This clause's target is not silence — it is §566, a statute Congress could amend tomorrow, and the parent's own "Inherently statutory" list names "moving/structuring the US Marshals Service." The case that a statutory fix is repealable by the majority that would repeal it is real, but that is answer 3 applied to a body that has never tried.
- *Symmetry (§5.2)* — **passes on its face, thin on its record.** The text names no officer, party, or era. But §5.2 asks whether it can be *sold* with each side's abuses paired, and the only legislative expression is single-party while the contempt record shows execution was never the failure point under either party.
- *Existential-problem (§5.1)* — **passes through the parent.** If judgments against the executive are unenforceable at the executive's option, judicial review is advisory. The open question is whether this clause is a material part of the answer.

## 4. Poll question

Field it in the same instrument as the anchor, with Marquette's obey-the-Court item carried unchanged as the benchmark. Name no incumbent; put the counterargument in the follow-up rather than burying it.

**Core item (verbatim):**

> *"Today, the officers who carry out federal court orders — serving them, and enforcing them when someone refuses — work for the Justice Department, part of the executive branch. So when a court rules against the executive branch, the officers who would enforce the ruling report to the same branch the ruling is against. Some people say these officers should instead report to the courts, so that no future president can block enforcement of a ruling against his own administration. Others say officers taking orders from judges rather than elected officials would give courts too much power. Which comes closer to your view?"*

Response options: *officers who enforce court orders should report to the courts* / *they should continue to report to the executive branch* / *not sure*.

**Follow-ups:**
1. *Costs named (the depth test, and the clause's central objection):* **"If officers reporting to judges could arrest or detain senior executive branch officials — including, in principle, cabinet secretaries — on a judge's order, would you still favor moving them under the courts, or not?"**
2. *The narrower variant (the clause's second sentence alone):* **"Suppose instead that these officers stayed in the executive branch, but were forbidden to take direction about enforcing a court order from any official whose own conduct is the subject of that order. Would you favor or oppose that rule?"**
3. *Symmetry check:* **"Would your answer be different depending on which party held the presidency?"** — report the crosstab, not the topline.
4. *Benchmark anchor (do not reword):* Marquette's item — **"If the Supreme Court rules against the president, is the president required to do as the ruling says, or can the president ignore a ruling he disagrees with?"**

**Threshold: a bipartisan floor of ≥60% on the core item, with the costs-named follow-up holding at ≥50% in both parties.** Both, not either. §6.4 records that the gate "systematically strips enforcement machinery," and the balanced-budget casualty is the precedent for accepting an abstract number without a costed one (69–80% abstract, 31% costed). If the core clears 60% in both parties but the costed follow-up falls below 50% in either, the public supports the idea and not the mechanism, and the clause stays held.

Report follow-up 2 separately against the same ≥60% floor. If the narrower variant outperforms the core item, that is the clause worth drafting — it removes the conflicted officer's direction without creating a judicial corps at all, which is the design goal the parent names and does not reach.

## 5. Recommendation

**Keep it as a candidate clause, but commission the drafting work alongside the survey rather than waiting on the survey alone.** This is the one held-back clause where a good poll result would not close the gate: the parent's trap note concedes the circularity never fully closes, and no threshold clears a design problem. The registry's open question 2 asks whether this clause should be re-filed as rejected-with-a-path-back for lacking a gate; it now has both a gate and a number, so it stays a candidate.

The work to pair with the poll is variant 2 — the conflict-of-interest sentence standing alone, without the branch transfer. It survives the trap note, it is severable as the parent's package skeleton requires, and it creates no judicial corps and no new office. If both variants come back with a Republican floor below 45%, the clause should move to rejected with the parent's statutory list as its path back, since Congress can do the whole of it by amending §566.
