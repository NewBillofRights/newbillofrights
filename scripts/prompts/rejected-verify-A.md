### BALANCED_BUDGET_REJECTED.md
1. **Missing Evidence**: The memo claims 49 states have balanced budget requirements to support the "states do it" framing, but fails to note the critical distinction: state BBRs generally apply only to *operating* budgets, not capital budgets, whereas the federal BBA applies to all outlays. This is the strongest counterargument to the state-analogue framing. *Source: National Association of State Budget Officers (NASBO) or National Conference of State Legislatures (NCSL).* CONFIDENCE HIGH.
2. **Factual Error**: The memo states H.J.Res. 2 (112th Congress) "dropped the tax supermajority and the cap." This is incorrect. H.J.Res. 2 in 2011 (which received 261 votes) *did* require a three-fifths supermajority to increase the debt limit and a two-thirds supermajority to raise taxes; it was the legislative vehicle for the "Cut, Cap, and Balance" pledge. *Source: Congress.gov, H.J.Res. 2 (112th Congress).* CONFIDENCE HIGH.
3. **Missing Evidence**: The memo relies on a 2011 Fox News poll for its primary "trade-off" rejection. It misses more recent trade-off polling that explicitly asks about balancing the budget if it requires cutting Medicare/Social Security, which shows massive bipartisan opposition and updates the 2011 data. *Source: Quinnipiac University National Poll (e.g., Feb 2023) or AP-NORC.* CONFIDENCE HIGH.

### COURT_EXPANSION_REJECTED.md
1. **Factual Error**: The memo states "Republican-appointed justices have been a majority of the Court continuously since the 1970–71 term except for fourteen months in 2016–17". This is factually wrong. Following Justice Scalia's death in February 2016 until Justice Gorsuch's seating in April 2017, the Court had 8 members and was tied 4–4. A 4–4 tie is not a Democratic-appointed majority. *Source: Supreme Court of the United States (Justices timeline).* CONFIDENCE HIGH.
2. **Missing Evidence**: The memo evaluates the "why-an-amendment" test for the *Keep Nine* amendment but fails to mention the strongest historical counterargument: FDR's 1937 Judicial Procedures Reform Bill ("court-packing plan"). The political failure of the 1937 plan established the norm that Keep Nine advocates argue must now be constitutionalized to prevent future statutory breaches. *Source: Senate Judiciary Committee Report, 1937.* CONFIDENCE HIGH.
3. **Partisan Tone**: "...the foundation has routed it to where both parties already agree..." This reads as the foundation taking a side on the merits of *how* to reform the Court, rather than neutrally evaluating the expansion proposal.
