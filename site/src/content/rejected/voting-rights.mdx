## The proposal

The Constitution forbids denying the vote on account of race, sex, or age, and bans poll taxes, but contains no affirmative right to vote. The proposal supplies one: "Every citizen of the United States, who is of legal voting age, shall have the fundamental right to vote in any public election held in the jurisdiction in which the citizen resides," enforceable by Congress.[^text] The current Senate version has nine cosponsors, none Republican, and no hearing.[^sjres186] Each party's statutory package shows what it would put behind the clause: one guarantees automatic and same-day registration, no-excuse mail voting, early-voting minimums, and restored preclearance; the other requires documentary proof of citizenship to register and government photo identification to vote.[^packages] A third variant writes mechanisms into state constitutions by ballot measure.[^state-model]

## The evidence

Access mechanisms draw a majority overall and a minority of Republicans; verification mechanisms, other than photo identification, draw a majority overall and, once a bill is on the floor, a minority of Democrats. The widest gap Pew has measured on any voting item is no-excuse mail voting: 58% overall, 32% of Republicans and 83% of Democrats (Pew Research Center, 2025).[^pew-mail]

Verification is the mirror image. Requiring first-time registrants to prove citizenship drew 66% of Democrats, 96% of Republicans, and 84% of independents (Gallup, 2024).[^gallup-poc] Eighteen months later, with a proof-of-citizenship bill on the Senate floor, it drew 37% of Democrats, 93% of Republicans, and 67% of independents (NPR/PBS News/Marist, 2026).[^marist-poc]

- Automatic registration: 42% of Republicans and 77% of Democrats; same-day registration: 43% and 72% (Pew Research Center, 2025).[^pew-reg]
- Photo identification to vote: 95% of Republicans and 71% of Democrats (Pew Research Center, 2025); 93% and 51% in a binary format (NPR/PBS News/Marist, 2026).[^photo-id]
- Forced to choose between ensuring everyone who wants to vote can and making sure no one ineligible votes, Democrats chose access 86 to 14 and Republicans chose eligibility 70 to 30 (NPR/PBS News/Marist, 2026).[^marist-forced]

Ballots and roll calls confirm the pattern. Standalone photo-identification amendments passed by wide margins in Nevada (73%, 2024) and Wisconsin (62.8%, 2025); identification bundled with absentee and drop-box limits lost in Maine, 64.2% to 35.8% (2025); Michigan's access package passed at 60.0% (2022) with an identification-or-affidavit clause.[^ballots] Every federal voter-access or verification package since 2021 has been a party-line vote.[^rollcalls]

## The test it failed

The bipartisan floor is 32%, among Republicans, on no-excuse mail voting — Weak. The verification package mirrors it: 37% of Democrats on proof of citizenship (NPR/PBS News/Marist, 2026) — Weak. The affirmative-right text itself is Unpolled. Four failure modes apply.

**High topline, low floor.** Mail voting and automatic registration poll at 58–60% overall and 32–43% among Republicans; proof of citizenship polls at 62–83% overall and 37–66% among Democrats.[^pew-mail][^marist-poc]

**Support moves with who is proposing.** Republican support for mail voting fell from 49% to 32% between 2020 and 2025 (Pew Research Center);[^pew-mail] Democratic support for photo identification rose from 61% to 71% between 2023 and 2025 (Pew Research Center) and stood at 51% once identification was a Republican floor vote (NPR/PBS News/Marist, 2026).[^photo-id]

**Framing dependency.** The framings that clear are mechanisms neither party's amendment would draft, and the amendment framing itself costs Democratic support on identification: 71% as policy (Pew Research Center, 2025) against 58% as a constitutional amendment, with 80% of Republicans, in a survey commissioned by the Cato Institute (Cato/Morning Consult, 2026).[^photo-id]

**Symmetry.** Each party's package is a remedy for the other's conduct — restored preclearance and overridden state identification rules on one side, citizenship documentation and federal roll matching on the other — and each side denies the other's premise. No package on the record asks both sides to give up something at the same moment.

## Framings we tested

Some mechanisms clear. Early in-person voting of at least two weeks: 71% of Republicans and 89% of Democrats (Pew Research Center, 2025) — Strong.[^gallup-early] An Election Day holiday, 74% and 76%, and paper backup for voting machines, 87% and 82% (Pew Research Center, 2025) — Strong, and both statutory administration.[^pew-admin] Photo identification clears as policy on the pre-2026 series and reads Moderate on the 2026 series — unstable, and a mechanism rather than a principle.[^photo-id] The paired package — an identification guarantee and an access guarantee in one clause — has never been polled; its nearest real test is Michigan, whose 60% carried an affidavit alternative rather than a strict identification rule.[^ballots] No framing on the record clears the bar as an amendment either party would sign.

## The path back

We will reopen the category if a single paired proposal — every citizen has the right to vote in federal elections; states may require free government-issued photo identification; every citizen has at least two weeks of early in-person voting and a paper record of the ballot — polled independently as one item, shows the lower party at 60% or above with an under-20-point gap in two polls; or if a joint resolution with that text is cosponsored by both parties and given a hearing; or if a swing-state ballot measure pairing a real identification requirement with an early-voting guarantee passes at 60% or more.
